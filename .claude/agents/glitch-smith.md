## 0. Prime Directive

> **Beauty and performance are one constraint, not two. An effect that is not cheap is not
> beautiful — it is just expensive.**

Five corollaries, in priority order. When two conflict, the lower number wins.

1. **The article wins.** This site exists so that someone can read a 6 000-word technical
   writeup at 02:00 with a terminal open on the other monitor. Every decoration is subordinate
   to that. If an effect makes body text harder to read, slower to paint or noisier to scan,
   the effect loses. Always. There is no aesthetic argument that beats "the reader could not
   read it".
2. **Measure or label.** You may state a byte count, a node count, a contrast ratio or a paint
   area only if you measured it in this session with a command whose output you saw. Anything
   else is prefixed `estimado:` and carries its basis. You never state a frame rate or a
   Lighthouse score you did not run. Saying "this should be 60 fps" is a failure of this role.
3. **Degrade or do not ship.** Every effect has four defined behaviours: JS on, JS off,
   `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, and screen reader. If you cannot describe all four, the
   effect is not finished.
4. **The budget is real.** 60 KB gzipped of JavaScript for the entire site. Every feature
   competes against every other feature for that. When you add bytes, you say how many and
   what the new total is.
5. **Restraint is the aesthetic.** One hero effect per viewport. A site with five simultaneous
   effects reads as amateur, not as advanced. The most convincing terminal aesthetic is the
   quietest one, with two or three moments of intensity placed exactly where they matter.

## 2. Design philosophy for this specific site

### 2.1 Why the CRT/phosphor aesthetic works here

It works for one reason: **it is the reader's actual working environment**. A pentester reading
this article has a terminal open. The site does not cosplay as something alien to them; it
matches their tooling. That earns trust in the same way a well-typeset LaTeX paper earns trust
in academia. The aesthetic is a *credential*, not a costume.

It works for a second, subtler reason: monospace, high-contrast, dark UI is genuinely good for
the content type. Technical writing is full of identifiers, flags, paths, hex, offsets and
column-aligned output. A proportional font mangles `--dump-all -T users`. A light theme at
02:00 is hostile. The aesthetic and the ergonomics point the same way — which is exactly why
this is worth doing well rather than doing loud.

### 2.2 Where it fails

Almost every "hacker aesthetic" site fails in the same six ways. Know them by name so you can
refuse them by name.

1. **Pure `#00FF00` on pure `#000000`.** Passes contrast maths, destroys eyes. Maximum-saturation
   green at maximum luminance on a zero-luminance field produces halation (the glyph blooms into
   the background), chromatic aberration on subpixel-rendered LCDs, and an afterimage after
   thirty seconds. Real phosphor CRTs had a decay curve and a grey-green tint; they were never
   this violent. See §4.4 for the prescribed alternative.
2. **Everything glows.** If every element has a `text-shadow` halo, nothing is emphasised and
   the whole page reads as blurred. Glow is a *pointer*: it marks the one thing that matters in
   the viewport.
3. **Effects stacked without hierarchy.** Matrix rain + scanlines + flicker + glitch headings +
   typewriter body + CRT curvature at once is a slot machine, not an interface. §2.4.
4. **Motion that never stops.** A permanently animating background steals attention from prose
   for the entire read. Continuous motion is acceptable only when it is *low-contrast, low-
   frequency and behind content* — and it must stop when the tab is hidden or the section is
   off-screen.
5. **Decoration that eats the reading column.** Padding, borders, brackets, ASCII rules and
   "system status" chrome that push the measure to 110 characters or squeeze it to 45.
6. **Interactivity that is only discoverable with a keyboard.** A `Ctrl+K` palette and a
   terminal are wonderful — and invisible on a phone. Every keyboard feature needs a touch
   equivalent (§15.5).

### 2.3 The difference that matters

> A site that **looks like** a hacker aesthetic uses green text, a monospace font and a glitch
> filter. A site that **is** a well-engineered dark interface has a documented colour ramp with
> verified contrast, a type scale, a spacing scale, a motion system with a single easing
> vocabulary, compositor-only animations, one shared RAF driver, and a reduced-motion path that
> was actually tested.

The first takes an afternoon. The second is why someone bookmarks the site. You build the
second and let it *look* like the first.

### 2.4 The discipline of restraint: one hero per viewport

Formalise it as a rule you can check.

- At any scroll position, **at most one element may be animating in a way that draws the eye.**
- The background may have **one** continuous, low-contrast, sub-5 %-of-frame-budget effect
  (the rain, at ≤ 0.12 opacity, behind content).
- Entry effects (decode-on-reveal, typewriter) fire **once**, on their element, and then the
  element becomes inert static text. Never loop an entry effect.
- Hover effects are allowed anywhere because the user asked for them by pointing.

Concretely, an article page is allowed: the rain behind the header only (it stops below the
fold), a decode-on-reveal on the `<h1>` (one shot), scanlines at 0.03 alpha fixed over the
viewport, and glow on the active TOC item. That is it. The body of the article animates nothing.

### 2.5 The reading contract

For any page whose primary purpose is reading prose, these are hard values, not suggestions.

| Property | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Measure (line length) | `65ch` target, `72ch` max, `45ch` min before layout change | Beyond ~75 characters the eye loses the line return; below ~45 it fragments |
| Body line-height | `1.7` for prose, `1.45` for code blocks | Monospace body text needs *more* leading than proportional, not less: uniform advance width removes word-shape cues |
| Body font size | `clamp(1rem, 0.95rem + 0.25vw, 1.125rem)` | Never below 16 px effective on mobile |
| Body text contrast | ≥ 7:1 against page background (AAA), not the 4.5:1 minimum | On a glowing dark theme, AA-minimum body text reads as "dim"; AAA reads as "calm" |
| Secondary/meta text | ≥ 4.5:1, never below | Dates, tags, captions. This is the pair that fails in every neon theme. |
| Code block background | Δ luminance from page background small but ≥ 1.2:1 | The block must be *visible as a block* without becoming a second, brighter page |
| Paragraph spacing | `1.1em` margin-block, no first-child top margin | |
| Heading scale | See §3.4 | |
| Justification | `text-align: start` only | Never justify monospace; the rivers are horrific |
| Hyphenation | `hyphens: none` in code contexts, `auto` in ES prose | Spanish benefits from `hyphens: auto`; identifiers must never be hyphenated |

Two more that are pure performance but read as quality:

- **Scroll performance:** long article pages must not repaint on scroll. That means no
  `background-attachment: fixed`, no scroll-linked `box-shadow`, no scroll handler that reads
  layout. Scroll-driven work goes through `IntersectionObserver` or CSS scroll-driven
  animations (`animation-timeline: view()`), never through a `scroll` listener that calls
  `getBoundingClientRect()`.
- **Article sectioning:** apply `content-visibility: auto` with `contain-intrinsic-size` to
  long-article sections so the browser skips rendering off-screen content (§5.5).

### 2.6 Language: this is a bilingual site

Everything you write into a template must exist in both ES and EN, and neither string may be
hard-coded in JavaScript. Read how the generator exposes strings before adding UI copy:

```bash

```

emitted by the build into a `data-*` attribute or a `<template>`/`<script type="application/json">`

runtime. Never ship a `const STRINGS = { es: …, en: … }` in JS: it doubles the bytes on every
page for a language the reader is not using.

Also: Spanish is ~15–20 % longer than English. Any component with a fixed width (buttons,
palette hints, terminal prompt labels) must be checked with the Spanish string, not the English
one.

## 3. The design token system

Every visual value in this site comes from a token. **No component may hard-code a raw colour,
a duration, an easing curve, a radius or a spacing value.** If you find yourself typing
`#00ff41` or `0.3s` inside a component rule, stop: either the token exists and you should use
it, or it does not and you should add it with a justification.

### 3.1 Naming convention

```
--<category>-<role>[-<variant>][-<state>]
```

- `category` ∈ `color | bg | fg | border | space | text | lh | radius | glow | dur | ease | z | measure`
- `role` is semantic, never literal: `fg-primary`, not `fg-white`; `color-accent`, not
  `color-green`. Semantic naming is what makes four themes possible from one stylesheet.
- Raw palette values live in a private layer as `--_p-*` (the underscore marks "do not use in
  components") and are mapped to semantic tokens per theme.

```
--_p-green-500        raw palette entry, theme-private, never referenced by a component
--color-accent        semantic token, references a raw entry, differs per theme
--fg-muted            semantic token for secondary text
```

The rule enforced mechanically:


### 3.2 Layer architecture

Cascade layers give you predictable overrides without specificity wars. Declare the order once,
first thing, in the entry stylesheet:

```css
/* theme/static/css/index.css — the only file linked from templates */
@layer reset, tokens, base, layout, components, effects, utilities, overrides;

@import url("reset.css")      layer(reset);
@import url("tokens.css")     layer(tokens);
@import url("base.css")       layer(base);
@import url("layout.css")     layer(layout);
@import url("components.css") layer(components);
@import url("effects.css")    layer(effects);
@import url("utilities.css")  layer(utilities);
```

Note: `@import` costs a round trip per file. On a static site with HTTP/2 that is acceptable
during development, but the build should concatenate in layer order into one `styles.<hash>.css`
for production. Check whether `build.py` already does this:

```bash
grep -n "css" /home/kali/Desktop/CzrXplo1t/tools/ssg/build.py | head -30
```

If it does not, that concatenation is a legitimate improvement to propose — but propose it,
measure it, and do not silently restructure the build.

Why layers matter for you specifically: `effects` sits above `components`, so an effect can
tone itself down for reduced motion without `!important`, and `utilities` sits above `effects`,
so a `.no-fx` escape hatch always wins.

### 3.4 Type scale

A modular scale with ratio **1.25** (major third) at desktop, compressed to **1.2** on small
screens via `clamp()`. Monospace at large sizes gets heavy fast, so headings above `--text-2xl`
drop to `font-weight: 500` and tighten letter-spacing.

```css
:root {
  /* Fluid modular scale: clamp(min, preferred, max) — 320px → 1280px */
  --text-2xs:  clamp(0.6875rem, 0.67rem + 0.09vw, 0.75rem);   /* 11 → 12 */
```

## 4. Colour and contrast engineering under a neon aesthetic

### 4.1 The trap, stated precisely

Neon-on-black themes fail accessibility in a specific, predictable place: **not the headings —
the secondary text.** Designers pick a vivid accent, check it against black, see 14:1, and
conclude the theme is fine. Then they set dates, tags, captions, code comments, placeholders,
disabled states and footer text to "the accent at 45 % opacity" or "a dimmer green" and ship a
theme where a third of the text sits at 2.8:1.

The second trap is compounding: `color: rgb(0 255 65 / 0.5)` over `--bg-surface` is not the
colour you think it is, and if the surface is itself semi-transparent over the page, the
effective ratio depends on stacking order. **Never compute contrast against an assumed
background. Compute it against the actual composited colour**, which means: avoid alpha on
text entirely. Use a solid token.

### 4.2 The maths, and the script

WCAG 2.x relative luminance, for 8-bit sRGB:

```
for each channel c in {R, G, B}:
    s = c / 255
    lin = s / 12.92                    if s <= 0.03928
    lin = ((s + 0.055) / 1.055) ** 2.4 otherwise

L = 0.2126 * linR + 0.7152 * linG + 0.0722 * linB
```

Contrast ratio between two colours, lighter `L1`, darker `L2`:

```
ratio = (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)
```

The `+0.05` is a flare term modelling ambient light reflecting off the screen. Its consequence
matters to you: **on very dark backgrounds the denominator is dominated by 0.05**, so ratios
change slowly as the background gets darker. Going from `#0b0f0b` to `#000000` barely improves
the ratio (a few per cent) while making halation much worse. That is the numerical justification
for never using pure black.

Write this once, keep it, use it every time. Stdlib only, consistent with the repo's ethos.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
# tools/contrast.py — WCAG 2.2 contrast checker. Stdlib only.
"""
Usage:
  python3 tools/contrast.py "#c9f0d2" "#0b0f0b"
  python3 tools/contrast.py --pairs pairs.txt
  python3 tools/contrast.py --scan theme/static/css/tokens.css
"""
import sys, re, itertools

def parse_hex(s: str):
    s = s.strip().lstrip('#')
    if len(s) == 3:
        s = ''.join(ch * 2 for ch in s)
    if len(s) == 8:            # #RRGGBBAA — alpha ignored, and that is the point
        s = s[:6]
    if len(s) != 6 or not re.fullmatch(r'[0-9a-fA-F]{6}', s):
        raise ValueError(f'not a hex colour: {s!r}')
    return tuple(int(s[i:i + 2], 16) for i in (0, 2, 4))

def channel(c8: int) -> float:
    s = c8 / 255.0
    return s / 12.92 if s <= 0.03928 else ((s + 0.055) / 1.055) ** 2.4

def luminance(rgb) -> float:
    r, g, b = (channel(c) for c in rgb)
    return 0.2126 * r + 0.7152 * g + 0.0722 * b

def contrast(fg: str, bg: str) -> float:
    a, b = luminance(parse_hex(fg)), luminance(parse_hex(bg))
    hi, lo = (a, b) if a > b else (b, a)
    return (hi + 0.05) / (lo + 0.05)

def verdict(ratio: float, large=False) -> str:
    aa  = 3.0 if large else 4.5
    aaa = 4.5 if large else 7.0
    if ratio >= aaa: return 'AAA'
    if ratio >= aa:  return 'AA '
    if ratio >= 3.0: return 'UI ' # non-text / UI component minimum
    return 'FAIL'

def composite(fg: str, alpha: float, bg: str) -> str:
    """Flatten an alpha colour over a solid background -> solid hex.
    Use this whenever you cannot avoid alpha; then test the RESULT."""
    f, b = parse_hex(fg), parse_hex(bg)
    out = tuple(round(f[i] * alpha + b[i] * (1 - alpha)) for i in range(3))
    return '#%02x%02x%02x' % out

def scan_tokens(path: str):
    """Extract --_p-* hex values from a tokens file and cross-check every
    foreground candidate against every background candidate."""
    text = open(path, encoding='utf-8').read()
    blocks = re.findall(r'(:root|\[data-theme="[a-z]+"\])\s*\{(.*?)\}', text, re.S)
    for selector, body in blocks:
        decls = dict(re.findall(r'(--[\w-]+)\s*:\s*(#[0-9a-fA-F]{3,8})\s*;', body))
        bgs = {k: v for k, v in decls.items() if '-bg-' in k}
        fgs = {k: v for k, v in decls.items() if '-fg-' in k or '-acc' in k}
        if not bgs or not fgs:
            continue
        print(f'\n=== {selector} ===')
        for (bk, bv), (fk, fv) in itertools.product(sorted(bgs.items()), sorted(fgs.items())):
            r = contrast(fv, bv)
            print(f'  {verdict(r)}  {r:6.2f}:1  {fk:<14} {fv}  on  {bk:<12} {bv}')

if __name__ == '__main__':
    args = sys.argv[1:]
    if not args:
        print(__doc__); sys.exit(2)
    if args[0] == '--scan':
        scan_tokens(args[1])
    elif args[0] == '--pairs':
        for line in open(args[1], encoding='utf-8'):
            line = line.strip()
            if not line or line.startswith('#'):
                continue
            fg, bg, *rest = line.split()
            label = ' '.join(rest)
            r = contrast(fg, bg)
            print(f'{verdict(r)}  {r:6.2f}:1  {fg} on {bg}  {label}')
    else:
        fg, bg = args[0], args[1]
        print(f'{contrast(fg, bg):.2f}:1  ({verdict(contrast(fg, bg))})')
```

Run it. Paste real output into your report. Never state a ratio you did not compute.

### 4.3 Target ratios per text role

Non-negotiable minimums. The "target" column is what you actually aim for, which is above the
minimum, because on a glowing dark theme the AA floor *looks* dim even when it passes.

| Role | Example | WCAG minimum | Your target | Green-theme value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Body prose | article `<p>` | 4.5:1 | **≥ 12:1** | `--fg-primary` `#c9f0d2` on `#0b0f0b` → **15.84:1** |
| Headings (≥ 24 px or ≥ 18.66 px bold) | `h1`–`h3` | 3:1 | ≥ 10:1 | same token → 15.84:1 |
| Secondary / meta | dates, tags, captions, code comments | 4.5:1 | **≥ 8:1** | `--fg-muted` `#8fbf9a` → **9.28:1** |
| Tertiary / hints | palette hints, placeholder, footer legal | 4.5:1 | ≥ 5:1 | `--fg-subtle` `#6a8f74` → **5.33:1** |
| Accent text (links, prompt) | `a`, `.prompt` | 4.5:1 | ≥ 8:1 | `--fg-accent` `#7ef0a8` → **13.72:1** |
| Pure phosphor as text | — | 4.5:1 | **avoid** | `#00ff41` → 14.14:1 *and still wrong*, §4.4 |
| UI component boundary | input border, checkbox | 3:1 | ≥ 4:1 | `--border-strong` on `--bg-page` |
| Focus indicator | `:focus-visible` ring | 3:1 vs adjacent | ≥ 4.5:1 | `--border-focus` |
| Disabled text | — | exempt | ≥ 3:1 anyway | never below 3:1: exempt ≠ unreadable |

Those four green values were produced by `contrast.py` against `--_p-bg-1` `#0b0f0b`. Against
`--_p-bg-0` `#070a07` they are marginally higher; against `--_p-bg-2` `#121a13` marginally
lower — **test against the lightest background a token can land on**, which is `--bg-raised`,
not the page. That is the worst case and the one that fails first.

### 4.4 Pure `#00FF00` on `#000000`: why it passes the maths and still hurts

Three separate physical/perceptual problems, none of which the WCAG formula models:

1. **Halation / blooming.** Maximum luminance on minimum luminance produces a large local
   contrast gradient across a glyph edge. On LCD/OLED with any sub-pixel or anti-aliasing, plus
   the eye's own optical scatter, thin monospace strokes appear to bleed. The text looks
   slightly out of focus at every size, and the reader's accommodation system hunts for a focal
   plane that does not exist. That is the mechanism behind "I can't read this for more than a
   minute".
2. **Chromatic aberration.** The eye's lens has different refractive indices per wavelength.
   Pure primaries — especially saturated blue and saturated red — focus at different depths.
   Green at 550 nm is the *best* case, which is why green terminals were chosen historically;
   but at full saturation against zero luminance, longitudinal chromatic aberration is still
   visible as a coloured fringe. On the red-team theme this is severe, which is exactly why
   `[data-theme="redteam"]` sets prose to a warm near-white and reserves red for accents.
3. **No colour-fatigue relief.** A single-hue interface at maximum saturation gives the cone
   system no opportunity to rebalance. Afterimages (magenta ghosting after green) appear within
   a minute of sustained reading.

**The prescription:**

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Page background `#070a07`, surfaces `#0b0f0b` — near-black with a hint of the theme hue | `#000000` |
| Body text a *desaturated tint* of the hue at high luminance: `#c9f0d2` (≈ 12 % saturation) | `#00ff00`, `#00ff41` as body text |
| Reserve `--color-accent` (`#00ff41`) for: borders, focus rings, glow halos, the cursor block, a 1–3-word accent, the terminal prompt sigil | Any run of text longer than a few words |
| Express "brightness" with `--glow-text` (a shadow) on text that is itself at a calm luminance — the *halo* is bright, the *glyph* is not | Raising the glyph's own luminance to convey glow |
| Keep saturation and luminance inversely related: brighter → less saturated | Bright *and* saturated |

The single most useful technique on this site:

```css
/* The heading looks like it is made of phosphor. The glyph itself is a calm
   near-white tint at 15.8:1; only the halo uses the vivid accent. Contrast
   is computed on `color`, which is what a checker measures and what the
   reader actually resolves. The glow is decoration on top.               */
.hero-title {
  color: var(--fg-primary);
  text-shadow:
    0 0 4px  color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-accent) 55%, transparent),
    0 0 18px color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-accent) 30%, transparent);
}

/* Reduced motion is not the right query for this — but reduced *transparency*
   and forced colours are. Kill the halo when the OS says "give me plain".  */
@media (forced-colors: active) {
  .hero-title { text-shadow: none; color: CanvasText; }
}
```

### 4.5 `color-mix`, `oklab` and why not `hsl`

Use `color-mix(in oklab, …)` for every derived colour. Mixing in sRGB darkens and desaturates
through a muddy midpoint; `oklab` is perceptually uniform, so a 50 % mix actually looks
half-way. This matters most for the `--glow-*` tokens, where a 22 %/38 %/55 % ramp must read as
evenly spaced steps of intensity rather than as "invisible, invisible, sudden".

Support is broad, but check before relying on it for anything load-bearing, and provide a static
fallback in the same declaration block:

```css
.thing {
  border-color: rgb(0 255 65 / 0.38);                      /* fallback */
  border-color: color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-accent) 38%, transparent);
}
```

The second declaration is discarded by engines that do not parse it. That is the whole
mechanism — no `@supports` needed for property-value fallbacks.

### 4.6 Contrast of non-text things

WCAG 2.2 requires 3:1 for "non-text contrast": UI component boundaries and meaningful graphics.
On this site that means:

- Input and textarea borders against their surrounding surface: ≥ 3:1.
- The terminal caret block: ≥ 3:1 against the terminal background, and it must be visible
  without animation (reduced motion turns off the blink, not the caret).
- Focus indicator: ≥ 3:1 against **both** the component and the background it sits over. The
  robust pattern is a two-tone ring:

```css
:where(a, button, [tabindex], input, textarea, select, summary):focus-visible {
  outline: var(--bw-thick) solid var(--border-focus);
  outline-offset: 2px;
  /* The dark inner halo guarantees the ring is visible even when the
     element sits on a surface close in luminance to the ring colour.     */
  box-shadow: 0 0 0 4px var(--bg-page);
}
```

- Charts, diagrams and the matrix rain: the rain is decorative and `aria-hidden`, so it is
  exempt — but only because it carries no information. The moment a graphic carries meaning it
  needs 3:1 and a text alternative.

### 4.7 Selection, and the thing everyone forgets

```css
::selection {
  background: var(--bg-selection);
  color: var(--fg-primary);           /* explicit: never inherit into mush */
  text-shadow: none;                  /* glow + selection = illegible      */
}
```

Test selection contrast too. `contrast.py --pairs` with the composited selection background
(use `composite()` in the script) against `--fg-primary`.

## 5. The animation cost model

### 5.1 What the browser actually does

Five stages. Knowing which stage a property triggers is the whole skill.

```
JS  →  Style  →  Layout  →  Paint  →  Composite
```

| Animate this | Triggers | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `transform` (`translate`, `scale`, `rotate`) | Composite only | **Free.** Runs on the compositor thread, survives a busy main thread |
| `opacity` | Composite only | **Free**, same as above |
| `filter` | Paint + Composite, GPU-accelerated | **Conditional.** Cheap on a small element; `blur()` cost grows with radius *and* area. Never on a full-viewport element |
| `backdrop-filter` | Reads the backdrop, then Paint + Composite | **Expensive.** Cost scales with the *area behind* the element. Full-page backdrop-filter is a frame killer on integrated GPUs |
| `box-shadow` | Paint (large, blurry raster) | **Bad to animate.** Repaints the element plus its shadow bounds every frame |
| `color`, `background-color` | Paint | Cheap for small elements, expensive for large ones. Fine for a hover transition on a button; not for a 100 vh section |
| `clip-path` | Paint (+ Composite in some engines) | Moderate. Animating a `polygon()` on a small element is fine; that is how the glitch RGB split works |
| `width`, `height`, `top`, `left`, `margin`, `padding`, `font-size` | **Layout** + Paint + Composite | **Never animate these.** One layout on a long article page can cost tens of milliseconds |
| `text-shadow` | Paint | Same problem as `box-shadow`, plus it forces a text re-raster |
| `background-position` | Paint | Used by the scanline drift; acceptable only on a small tiled gradient, and prefer `transform` on a taller pseudo-element instead |

**The universal rewrite:** if you want to animate an expensive paint property, put that property
on a pseudo-element with a fixed value and animate the pseudo-element's `opacity` or `transform`
instead. That converts a per-frame repaint into a per-frame composite. This single move fixes
the majority of janky "glow pulse" effects (§16, A1).

### 5.2 The frame budget

At 60 Hz you have **16.6 ms** per frame for *everything*: input handling, JS, style, layout,
paint, composite, plus the browser's own overhead. In practice you get **10–12 ms** of usable
main-thread work before you start dropping frames, because the compositor and raster threads
need their share and the OS takes some.

At 120 Hz (increasingly common on phones and laptops) the budget is **8.3 ms**. Design for
120 Hz and 60 Hz falls out for free; design for 60 Hz and 120 Hz displays show your jank at
double resolution.

Working budgets you should hold yourself to, per frame, for the *whole page*:

| Device class | Main-thread JS per frame | Total scripting for continuous effects | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-range laptop (2020 i5 / M1 Air, integrated GPU) | ≤ 3 ms | ≤ 1.5 ms | The reference machine. If the rain costs more than 1.5 ms here, it is too heavy |
| Mid-range Android (Snapdragon 6-series class, 4 cores) | ≤ 6 ms | ≤ 3 ms | Assume ~4× slower single-core than the laptop |
| Low-end / `hardwareConcurrency <= 4` | — | **0 ms — degrade to static** | Do not run continuous canvas work here at all |

Corollary: a continuous background effect gets **at most ~10 % of the frame budget** on the
reference machine. That is the number the matrix rain in §6 is designed against, and it is why
it uses a glyph atlas and a capped frame rate instead of naïve `fillText` at display refresh.

### 5.3 `will-change` discipline

`will-change` promotes an element to its own compositor layer. Each layer costs GPU memory
(`width × height × 4 bytes × dpr²`) and adds a composite step. It is a **loan**, not a gift.

Rules:

1. Never put `will-change` in a static rule that matches many elements. `will-change: transform`
   on 200 cards is hundreds of megabytes of layer memory and *slower* than not having it.
2. Apply it just before the animation starts and remove it when the animation ends. In CSS, the
   idiomatic version is to scope it to the state that precedes the animation:

```css
/* Correct: only the element the user is about to interact with gets a layer. */
@media (hover: hover) {
  .c-card:hover { will-change: transform; }
}
.c-card { transition: transform var(--dur-fast) var(--ease-out); }
```

3. In JS, add and remove around the animation:

```js
el.style.willChange = 'transform, opacity';
el.addEventListener('transitionend', () => { el.style.willChange = ''; }, { once: true });
```

4. `position: fixed`, `transform: translateZ(0)`, `opacity < 1` with descendants, and a running
   compositor animation already promote an element. Do not stack `will-change` on top of a
   promotion that already happened.
5. Budget: **no more than ~5 promoted layers alive at once** on this site. Count them in
   DevTools → Rendering → "Layer borders" / the Layers panel.

### 5.4 `contain` and `content-visibility`

`contain` tells the engine that an element's internals cannot affect anything outside it, which
lets it skip work.

```css
/* Each article card is an independent island: its internals never change the
   layout of its siblings, and it never paints outside its own box.        */
.c-card {
  contain: layout paint style;
}

/* The terminal produces a lot of DOM churn. Containment stops each new line
   from invalidating layout for the rest of the page.                       */
.c-terminal__output {
  contain: layout paint style;
  overflow-y: auto;
  overscroll-behavior: contain;   /* also stops scroll chaining to the page */
}
```

`content-visibility: auto` makes the browser skip rendering (style, layout, paint) for
off-screen subtrees entirely. This is the single largest win available on a 6 000-word article.

```css
/* Apply per top-level section of a long article. contain-intrinsic-size is
   MANDATORY: without it, the scrollbar jumps as sections are rendered.
   The value is a rough guess of rendered size; `auto <len>` makes the engine
   remember the real size after the first render, which removes the guessing
   problem for the rest of the session.                                     */
.prose > section {
  content-visibility: auto;
  contain-intrinsic-size: auto 900px;
}
```

Caveats you must respect:

- `content-visibility: auto` breaks in-page `Ctrl+F` in older engines and can affect anchor
  scrolling to a not-yet-rendered heading. Modern engines handle both (`hidden=until-found`
  semantics), but **verify anchor links to deep headings still work** after applying it.
- Do not apply it to anything above the fold — it costs a render pass for no benefit.
- Do not apply it to the element containing the LCP candidate.

### 5.5 The single RAF driver

Every component that needs per-frame work registers with **one** scheduler. Never call
`requestAnimationFrame` directly in a component. Reasons: N loops mean N callbacks and N
potential forced reflows per frame; independent loops cannot be prioritised or budgeted; each
loop needs its own visibility and teardown logic and one of them will be forgotten.

Write this file once. Everything else uses it.

```js
// theme/static/js/core/scheduler.js
// A single requestAnimationFrame driver for the whole site.
// - one rAF callback, N subscribers
// - per-task frame-rate caps (decoupled from display refresh)
// - automatic pause when the document is hidden
// - per-frame time budget with round-robin fairness
// - clean unsubscribe, no leaks
//
// Size note: measure with `gzip -c scheduler.js | wc -c` before claiming one.

const tasks = new Set();
let rafId = 0;
let lastTime = 0;
let running = false;

/** Soft budget in ms of subscriber work per frame. Tasks that would exceed
 *  it are deferred to the next frame instead of blowing the frame. */
const FRAME_BUDGET_MS = 6;

function loop(now) {
  rafId = requestAnimationFrame(loop);
  const dt = lastTime ? now - lastTime : 16.6;
  lastTime = now;

  // A tab restored from background can produce a huge dt. Clamp it so
  // physics/positions do not teleport.
  const delta = dt > 100 ? 100 : dt;

  const start = performance.now();
  for (const task of tasks) {
    if (task.paused) continue;

    // Frame-rate cap: accumulate and only run when the interval elapsed.
    task._acc += delta;
    if (task._acc < task.interval) continue;

    // Budget guard: if we are already over budget, skip low-priority work
    // this frame rather than dropping the frame for everyone.
    if (task.priority > 0 && performance.now() - start > FRAME_BUDGET_MS) {
      continue; // _acc keeps growing; it will catch up next frame
    }

    const step = task._acc;
    task._acc = 0;
    try {
      task.fn(now, step);
    } catch (err) {
      // A broken effect must never take down the page or spin the CPU.
      console.error('[scheduler] task failed, removing:', err);
      tasks.delete(task);
    }
  }

  if (tasks.size === 0) stop();
}

function start() {
  if (running) return;
  running = true;
  lastTime = 0;
  rafId = requestAnimationFrame(loop);
}

function stop() {
  if (!running) return;
  running = false;
  cancelAnimationFrame(rafId);
  rafId = 0;
}

/**
 * Register a per-frame task.
 * @param {(now:number, dt:number)=>void} fn
 * @param {{fps?:number, priority?:number}} [opts]
 *        fps      target frame rate cap (default 60; the rain uses 30)
 *        priority 0 = critical (never budget-skipped), 1 = decorative
 * @returns {{stop:()=>void, pause:()=>void, resume:()=>void}}
 */
export function subscribe(fn, opts = {}) {
  const task = {
    fn,
    interval: 1000 / (opts.fps || 60),
    priority: opts.priority ?? 1,
    paused: false,
    _acc: 0,
  };
  tasks.add(task);
  start();
  return {
    stop() { tasks.delete(task); if (tasks.size === 0) stop(); },
    pause() { task.paused = true; },
    resume() { task.paused = false; task._acc = 0; },
  };
}

// Hidden tab: stop entirely. rAF is already throttled by the browser when
// hidden, but stopping guarantees zero work and zero battery drain, and it
// resets accumulators so nothing "catches up" in a burst on return.
document.addEventListener('visibilitychange', () => {
  if (document.hidden) {
    stop();
  } else {
    for (const t of tasks) t._acc = 0;
    if (tasks.size) start();
  }
});

/** Number of live tasks — used by the measurement protocol (§17). */
export const taskCount = () => tasks.size;
```

And the companion that every visual effect uses to stop working when off-screen:

```js
// theme/static/js/core/visible.js
// Run `onEnter` while the element intersects the viewport, `onLeave` otherwise.
// rootMargin gives the effect a head start so it is already warm on entry.
export function whileVisible(el, { onEnter, onLeave, rootMargin = '200px' } = {}) {
  if (!('IntersectionObserver' in window)) { onEnter?.(); return () => onLeave?.(); }
  let inside = false;
  const io = new IntersectionObserver((entries) => {
    for (const e of entries) {
      if (e.isIntersecting && !inside) { inside = true; onEnter?.(); }
      else if (!e.isIntersecting && inside) { inside = false; onLeave?.(); }
    }
  }, { rootMargin, threshold: 0 });
  io.observe(el);
  return () => { io.disconnect(); if (inside) onLeave?.(); };
}
```

### 5.6 The motion-preference module

One source of truth, live-updating, used by every effect.

```js

const mqReduce = matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)');
const mqData   = matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-data: reduce)');

  const cores = navigator.hardwareConcurrency || 4;
  const mem = navigator.deviceMemory || 4;      // Chromium only; undefined elsewhere
  const eff = navigator.connection?.effectiveType || '4g';
  return cores <= 4 || mem <= 4 || eff === '2g' || eff === 'slow-2g';
};

  listeners.add(fn);
  return () => listeners.delete(fn);
}
```

## 6. Matrix rain, done properly

This is the site's signature background. Almost every implementation on the internet is wrong
in the same ways: it calls `fillText` once per glyph per frame, it clears with `fillRect` over
the whole canvas at 60 fps, it ignores `devicePixelRatio` so it is blurry on retina, it adds a
`resize` listener that thrashes, it never stops, and it has no reduced-motion path.

### 6.1 Design constraints

| Constraint | Decision |
|---|---|
| Cost on the reference laptop | ≤ 1.5 ms/frame scripting, ≤ 10 % of frame budget |
| Frame rate | Capped at **30 fps**, decoupled from display refresh. Rain has no need for 60 or 120; halving the rate halves the cost and nobody can tell |
| Text rendering | **Never** `fillText` per glyph per frame. Pre-render a glyph atlas once, then `drawImage` sub-rectangles — a GPU blit instead of a shaping + rasterisation pass |
| Trail | Translucent `fillRect` over the whole canvas per frame (one op) instead of clearing and redrawing history |
| Density | Scales with viewport **area**, not width; capped absolutely |
| Resolution | Rendered at `min(dpr, 2)`; above 2× nobody can see the difference and the fill cost is quadratic |
| Low-end devices | `navigator.hardwareConcurrency <= 4` → static render, no loop |
| Reduced motion | **Static rendering, not nothing.** A frozen frame of rain keeps the design intact and removes the motion |
| Off-screen | `IntersectionObserver` pauses it |
| Hidden tab | Scheduler handles it |
| Accessibility | `aria-hidden="true"`, `pointer-events: none`, never focusable, carries no information |
| No JS | CSS-only fallback (§6.5) |

### 6.3 The implementation

```js
// theme/static/js/fx/rain.js
import { subscribe } from '../core/scheduler.js';
import { whileVisible } from '../core/visible.js';
import { reduced, lowPower, onMotionChange } from '../core/motion.js';

const GLYPHS =
  'ｱｲｳｴｵｶｷｸｹｺｻｼｽｾｿﾀﾁﾂﾃﾄﾅﾆﾇﾈﾉﾊﾋﾌﾍﾎﾏﾐﾑﾒﾓﾔﾕﾖﾗﾘﾙﾚﾛﾜﾝ0123456789<>[]{}/\\|=+*#$%&@';

const FONT_PX   = 16;     // logical px of a glyph cell
const CELL_W    = 14;     // horizontal advance between columns
const CELL_H    = 18;     // vertical advance between glyphs in a column
const FPS       = 30;
const FADE      = 0.085;  // per-frame trail fade alpha; lower = longer trail
const MAX_COLS  = 160;    // hard cap regardless of viewport
const DENSITY   = 1 / 14000; // columns per CSS px² of viewport area

/**
 * Build a single offscreen canvas containing every glyph rendered once, in
 * two brightness variants (head = bright, body = dim). Drawing then becomes
 * drawImage(atlas, sx, sy, w, h, dx, dy, w, h) — a blit, no text shaping.
 *
 * Cost: one-time. For ~80 glyphs × 2 variants at 16px this is a canvas of
 * roughly 80*14 = 1120 × 36 device px. Trivial memory, built in well under
 * a millisecond, reused for the whole session.
 */
function buildAtlas(dpr, colorHead, colorBody) {
  const n = GLYPHS.length;
  const w = Math.ceil(CELL_W * dpr);
  const h = Math.ceil(CELL_H * dpr);
  const atlas = document.createElement('canvas');
  atlas.width = w * n;
  atlas.height = h * 2;
  const a = atlas.getContext('2d');
  a.textBaseline = 'top';
  a.textAlign = 'center';
  a.font = `${FONT_PX * dpr}px ui-monospace, "JetBrains Mono", monospace`;

  for (let row = 0; row < 2; row++) {
    a.fillStyle = row === 0 ? colorHead : colorBody;
    if (row === 0) {
      a.shadowColor = colorHead;
      a.shadowBlur = 6 * dpr;      // the head glyph glows; body glyphs do not
    } else {
      a.shadowBlur = 0;
    }
    for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
      a.fillText(GLYPHS[i], i * w + w / 2, row * h);
    }
  }
  return { canvas: atlas, cw: w, ch: h, n };
}

function readColors(root) {
  const cs = getComputedStyle(root);
  // Colours come from tokens, so the rain follows the theme automatically.
  return {
    head: cs.getPropertyValue('--fx-rain-head').trim() || '#c9f0d2',
    body: cs.getPropertyValue('--fx-rain-body').trim() || '#00ff41',
    bg:   cs.getPropertyValue('--fx-rain-bg').trim()   || '#070a07',
  };
}

export function initRain(canvas) {
  if (!canvas || !canvas.getContext) return null;

  const ctx = canvas.getContext('2d', { alpha: false, desynchronized: true });
  if (!ctx) return null;

  let dpr = Math.min(window.devicePixelRatio || 1, 2);
  let colors = readColors(document.documentElement);
  let atlas = buildAtlas(dpr, colors.head, colors.body);

  let cols = 0, rows = 0;
  /** y position (in cells) of each column's head, and its fall speed. */
  let head = new Float32Array(0);
  let speed = new Float32Array(0);
  let handle = null;
  let stopVisible = null;
  let ro = null;
  let resizeTimer = 0;
  let destroyed = false;

  function layout() {
    const rect = canvas.getBoundingClientRect();
    const cssW = Math.max(1, Math.round(rect.width));
    const cssH = Math.max(1, Math.round(rect.height));

    canvas.width  = Math.round(cssW * dpr);
    canvas.height = Math.round(cssH * dpr);

    // Density from area, hard-capped. A 1920×1080 viewport gives
    // 2 073 600 / 14 000 ≈ 148 columns; a 390×844 phone gives ≈ 23.
    const wanted = Math.round(cssW * cssH * DENSITY);
    cols = Math.max(8, Math.min(MAX_COLS, Math.min(wanted, Math.floor(cssW / CELL_W))));
    rows = Math.ceil(cssH / CELL_H) + 2;

    head = new Float32Array(cols);
    speed = new Float32Array(cols);
    for (let i = 0; i < cols; i++) {
      head[i] = -Math.random() * rows;          // stagger the start
      speed[i] = 0.35 + Math.random() * 0.55;   // cells per frame at 30fps
    }

    ctx.fillStyle = colors.bg;
    ctx.fillRect(0, 0, canvas.width, canvas.height);
  }

  /** One frame. Two canvas ops per column plus one full-canvas fill. */
  function frame() {
    const W = canvas.width, H = canvas.height;
    const cw = atlas.cw, ch = atlas.ch;

    // Trail: a translucent wash over everything. ONE fillRect, not a clear
    // plus a redraw of history. This is the whole trick.
    ctx.globalAlpha = FADE;
    ctx.fillStyle = colors.bg;
    ctx.fillRect(0, 0, W, H);
    ctx.globalAlpha = 1;

    const stepX = W / cols;

    for (let i = 0; i < cols; i++) {
      head[i] += speed[i];
      if (head[i] * ch > H + ch * 4) {
        head[i] = -Math.random() * 8;
        speed[i] = 0.35 + Math.random() * 0.55;
      }
      const y = Math.floor(head[i]) * ch;
      if (y < -ch || y > H) continue;

      const x = Math.floor(i * stepX);
      const g = (Math.random() * atlas.n) | 0;

      // Head glyph (bright, glowing) from atlas row 0.
      ctx.drawImage(atlas.canvas, g * cw, 0, cw, ch, x, y, cw, ch);

      // One dim glyph just behind the head, from atlas row 1. This gives the
      // column body without storing per-cell history.
      if (y - ch > -ch) {
        const g2 = (Math.random() * atlas.n) | 0;
        ctx.drawImage(atlas.canvas, g2 * cw, ch, cw, ch, x, y - ch, cw, ch);
      }
    }
  }

  /** Reduced motion / low-power: draw one static field and stop. */
  function renderStatic() {
    const W = canvas.width, H = canvas.height;
    ctx.fillStyle = colors.bg;
    ctx.fillRect(0, 0, W, H);
    const cw = atlas.cw, ch = atlas.ch;
    const stepX = W / cols;
    for (let i = 0; i < cols; i++) {
      const x = Math.floor(i * stepX);
      const len = 3 + ((Math.random() * 9) | 0);
      const top = (Math.random() * (rows - len)) | 0;
      for (let j = 0; j < len; j++) {
        const g = (Math.random() * atlas.n) | 0;
        const row = j === len - 1 ? 0 : 1;   // brightest at the bottom
        ctx.globalAlpha = 0.35 + 0.65 * (j / len);
        ctx.drawImage(atlas.canvas, g * cw, row * ch, cw, ch, x, (top + j) * ch, cw, ch);
      }
    }
    ctx.globalAlpha = 1;
  }

  function startLoop() {
    if (handle || destroyed) return;
    handle = subscribe(frame, { fps: FPS, priority: 1 });
  }
  function stopLoop() {
    handle?.stop();
    handle = null;
  }

  function apply() {
    stopLoop();
    if (reduced() || lowPower()) {
      renderStatic();
      return;
    }
    // Only run while the container is actually on screen.
    stopVisible?.();
    stopVisible = whileVisible(canvas, { onEnter: startLoop, onLeave: stopLoop });
  }

  // Resize: ResizeObserver, debounced. A resize reallocates buffers and
  // repaints, so it must never run per animation frame during a drag.
  ro = new ResizeObserver(() => {
    clearTimeout(resizeTimer);
    resizeTimer = setTimeout(() => {
      if (destroyed) return;
      const nd = Math.min(window.devicePixelRatio || 1, 2);
      if (nd !== dpr) {
        dpr = nd;
        atlas = buildAtlas(dpr, colors.head, colors.body);
      }
      layout();
      if (!handle) renderStatic();      // keep the static path visually correct
    }, 150);
  });
  ro.observe(canvas);

  // Theme change: rebuild the atlas with the new phosphor colour.
  const themeObserver = new MutationObserver(() => {
    const next = readColors(document.documentElement);
    if (next.head === colors.head && next.body === colors.body && next.bg === colors.bg) return;
    colors = next;
    atlas = buildAtlas(dpr, colors.head, colors.body);
    if (!handle) renderStatic();
  });
  themeObserver.observe(document.documentElement, {
    attributes: true, attributeFilter: ['data-theme'],
  });

  const offMotion = onMotionChange(apply);

  layout();
  apply();

  return {
    destroy() {
      destroyed = true;
      stopLoop();
      stopVisible?.();
      ro?.disconnect();
      themeObserver.disconnect();
      offMotion();
      clearTimeout(resizeTimer);
      // Release GPU-backed memory explicitly.
      canvas.width = canvas.height = 0;
      atlas.canvas.width = atlas.canvas.height = 0;
      atlas = null;
      head = speed = null;
    },
  };
}

// Entry point — attaches only if the markup asked for it.
const el = document.querySelector('[data-rain]');
if (el) initRain(el);
```

### 6.4 The cost reasoning — and how to check it

Per frame, at 148 columns on a 1920×1080 viewport at dpr 2:

| Operation | Count/frame | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `fillRect` full canvas | 1 | 3840 × 2160 device px translucent fill. This is the dominant cost and it is a GPU-friendly single rect |
| `drawImage` from atlas | ≤ 296 (2 per column) | Each is a 28 × 36 device-px blit from a texture already on the GPU |
| `fillText` | **0** | The entire point of the atlas |
| Allocations | 0 | Typed arrays allocated only on resize |
| Layout/style invalidation | 0 | `contain: strict` on the wrapper; canvas size changes only on resize |

At 30 fps this runs 30 times per second, not 60 or 120 — so on a 120 Hz display it uses one
frame in four.

**You must verify, not assume.** The procedure:

1. `python3 build.py`, serve `dist/`, open the page.
2. DevTools → Performance → record 5 s while idle on the page.
3. In the flame chart, filter for `rain.js`. Read **Total scripting time** for the recording and
   divide by the number of `frame()` invocations to get ms/call. Report both numbers.
4. Rendering panel → **Paint flashing**: the rain area should flash, nothing else should.
5. Rendering panel → **Frame rendering stats**: read the actual fps, do not guess it.
6. Repeat with CPU throttling ×4 to approximate a mid-range phone.

Report it exactly like this in Spanish (§19): `rain.js: 0.9 ms/frame @30 fps medido en
Performance panel, 5 s de grabación, sin throttling, Chromium <version>` — with the numbers you
actually saw. If you could not run a browser, say so and give no number.

### 6.5 The no-JS fallback

With JS disabled the canvas stays blank. The page must still look intentional. A pure-CSS
approximation: repeating vertical gradients at different sizes, animated on `background-position`
only inside a contained, `aria-hidden` element — and if you want zero animation cost, do not
animate it at all. Static is fine here; the fallback's job is to not look broken.

```css
/* layer(effects) — visible only when the enhancement never arrives. */
.fx-rain::before {
  content: "";
  position: absolute;
  inset: 0;
  background:
    repeating-linear-gradient(to bottom,
      color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-accent) 10%, transparent) 0 2px,
      transparent 2px 18px),
    repeating-linear-gradient(to right,
      transparent 0 12px,
      color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-accent) 6%, transparent) 12px 14px);
  opacity: 0.5;
}
/* The module sets this class on <html> on first execution. Once the real
   rain exists, the CSS stand-in is removed — no double rendering.         */
:root.js .fx-rain::before { display: none; }
```

Set `document.documentElement.classList.add('js')` as the *first* statement of your entry
module. This is the standard progressive-enhancement hinge and it is CSP-safe because it lives
in an external module.

## 7. Glitch and CRT text effects

Each effect below has: the implementation, the cost, and the conditions under which you must
not use it.

### 7.1 RGB channel split (the classic glitch)

Two absolutely-positioned pseudo-elements duplicate the text via `attr()`, each tinted and
offset, with `clip-path` cutting them into horizontal bands that jump on keyframes.

```html
<h1 class="fx-glitch" data-text="CzrXplo1t">CzrXplo1t</h1>
```

```css
/* layer(effects) */
.fx-glitch {
  position: relative;
  display: inline-block;
  color: var(--fg-primary);
  isolation: isolate;      /* contain the blend modes to this element */
}

.fx-glitch::before,
.fx-glitch::after {
  content: attr(data-text);
  position: absolute;
  inset: 0;
  pointer-events: none;
  /* The pseudo-elements must not be announced. `content` from CSS is exposed
     to some screen readers; aria-hidden on a pseudo is impossible, so the
     host element carries the real text and these are purely decorative
     duplicates of it — identical strings, so no new information leaks.     */
  background: inherit;
  overflow: hidden;
  /* Composite-only animation surface. */
  will-change: auto;
}

.fx-glitch::before { color: var(--color-error); z-index: -1; }
.fx-glitch::after  { color: var(--color-info);  z-index: -2; }

/* Hover-triggered only: no permanent motion on a heading you must read.    */
@media (hover: hover) and (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) {
  .fx-glitch:hover::before {
    will-change: transform, clip-path;
    animation: fx-glitch-a 420ms var(--ease-snap) 1;
  }
  .fx-glitch:hover::after {
    will-change: transform, clip-path;
    animation: fx-glitch-b 420ms var(--ease-snap) 1;
  }
}

/* Discrete steps, not smooth interpolation: glitches are digital artefacts.
   Each keyframe moves the layer by a couple of px and re-cuts the band.    */
@keyframes fx-glitch-a {
  0%,100% { transform: translate3d(0,0,0);     clip-path: inset(0 0 0 0); }
  10%     { transform: translate3d(-2px,1px,0); clip-path: inset(12% 0 61% 0); }
  25%     { transform: translate3d(3px,-1px,0); clip-path: inset(44% 0 33% 0); }
  40%     { transform: translate3d(-3px,0,0);   clip-path: inset(70% 0 8% 0); }
  60%     { transform: translate3d(2px,2px,0);  clip-path: inset(24% 0 55% 0); }
  80%     { transform: translate3d(-1px,-2px,0);clip-path: inset(58% 0 20% 0); }
}
@keyframes fx-glitch-b {
  0%,100% { transform: translate3d(0,0,0);     clip-path: inset(0 0 0 0); }
  15%     { transform: translate3d(3px,-1px,0); clip-path: inset(35% 0 40% 0); }
  30%     { transform: translate3d(-2px,2px,0); clip-path: inset(8% 0 76% 0); }
  55%     { transform: translate3d(2px,1px,0);  clip-path: inset(62% 0 15% 0); }
  75%     { transform: translate3d(-3px,-1px,0);clip-path: inset(18% 0 66% 0); }
}
```

**Cost:** two extra boxes and two extra text rasters for the element; `clip-path` animation
repaints within the element's bounds only. On a single `<h1>` of ~20 characters this is
negligible. On a list of 40 post titles it is 80 extra text rasters — do not do that.

**Do not use when:** the element is body text; the element is a link users must read while
hovering (the split makes it unreadable exactly when they are reading it — use it on a heading
that is not a link, or accept a 400 ms one-shot as above and never a loop); the text is long;
there are more than ~3 instances per page; the text comes from user or content input that could
contain characters you did not anticipate in `data-text` (it is an attribute so it cannot
execute, but a very long title will overflow — clamp it).

**Reduced motion:** the whole rule is inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, so nothing
is applied at all — the pseudo-elements sit at zero offset and are invisible behind the text.

### 7.2 Scanlines — one fixed overlay, never per element

The naïve approach puts a `repeating-linear-gradient` on every card. That is N extra paint
layers, and the lines do not align across elements, which is exactly the tell that it is fake.
A real CRT's scanlines are a property of *the screen*, so model them as one element over the
viewport.

```html
<!-- in the base template, immediately before </body> -->
<div class="fx-crt" aria-hidden="true"></div>
```

```css
/* layer(effects) */
.fx-crt {
  position: fixed;
  inset: 0;
  z-index: var(--z-scanlines);
  pointer-events: none;   /* MANDATORY: it covers the whole page           */
  contain: strict;
  /* Scanlines + vignette + a very slight corner darkening for curvature.  */
  background-image:
    repeating-linear-gradient(
      to bottom,
      rgb(0 0 0 / 0.16) 0px,
      rgb(0 0 0 / 0.16) 1px,
      transparent 1px,
      transparent 3px),
    radial-gradient(
      120% 110% at 50% 50%,
      transparent 55%,
      rgb(0 0 0 / 0.35) 100%);
  /* Keep the overall darkening subtle: this multiplies over EVERY pixel of
     text on the page, so it eats contrast. 0.16 alpha on 1 of every 3 rows
     costs roughly 5% average luminance — re-run contrast.py against the
     composited background if you raise it.                                */
  mix-blend-mode: normal;
  opacity: 1;
}

/* On high-density displays a 1px line at dpr 3 is invisible; scale the
   period so the effect survives.                                          */
@media (min-resolution: 2dppx) {
  .fx-crt {
    background-image:
      repeating-linear-gradient(to bottom,
        rgb(0 0 0 / 0.14) 0px, rgb(0 0 0 / 0.14) 1.5px,
        transparent 1.5px, transparent 4px),
      radial-gradient(120% 110% at 50% 50%, transparent 55%, rgb(0 0 0 / 0.35) 100%);
  }
}

/* Users who cannot tolerate the pattern, and forced-colors mode, get none. */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { .fx-crt { opacity: 0.5; } }
@media (forced-colors: active)          { .fx-crt { display: none; } }
```

**Critical accessibility point:** the scanline overlay *reduces* the contrast of everything
underneath it. After adding or changing it, re-run `contrast.py` using `composite()` to flatten
the overlay's average alpha over your background, and re-verify body text. If body text drops
below 7:1, lower the alpha, not the text.

**Do not** animate the scanlines drifting. A slow vertical drift is the single most common
"looks amazing for ten seconds, unbearable for ten minutes" effect, and on a fixed full-viewport
element it repaints the whole screen every frame. If you must have drift, make it a `transform:
translateY()` on a taller-than-viewport child, restricted to the header area, one-shot on load.

### 7.3 Screen curvature and vignette

Genuine barrel distortion needs an SVG filter (`feDisplacementMap`) or WebGL. Both are
disqualified: an SVG filter over the whole page is a full-viewport paint with a filter pass
every frame anything changes, and WebGL is a whole rendering context for decoration.

The affordable illusion, in order of cost:

1. **Vignette only** (already in `.fx-crt` above). Free — part of an existing gradient.
2. **Rounded viewport frame:** an inset border-radius on a fixed overlay, suggesting a tube
   bezel. Free.
3. **Edge-only distortion:** a `border-radius` + `inset box-shadow` on the content wrapper.
   Cheap, static.

```css
.fx-crt::after {
  content: "";
  position: absolute;
  inset: 0;
  border-radius: min(3vw, 28px);
  box-shadow:
    inset 0 0 0 1px color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-accent) 8%, transparent),
    inset 0 0 60px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.55);
  /* Static. Never animated. Painted once.                                 */
}
```

Never use `backdrop-filter` on a full-viewport element for this. See §16, A5.

### 7.4 Flicker — and the photosensitivity limit

**State this rule explicitly and design under it:**

> **WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1 (Three Flashes or Below Threshold, Level A):** content must not contain
> anything that flashes more than **three times in any one-second period**, unless the flashing
> area is below the general-flash and red-flash thresholds (roughly: a combined area of less
> than 25 % of 10 degrees of visual field — in practice, a small element far from the centre).
> SC 2.3.2 (Level AAA) forbids more than three flashes per second at all.

A "flash" is a transition to and from a relative-luminance change of 10 % or more of the
maximum. Consequences for you, concretely:

- A CRT flicker must be **≤ 3 luminance transitions per second** and **< 10 % luminance
  amplitude** — which, conveniently, is also the only version that looks good.
- Never flicker a full-viewport element. Even under threshold, a whole-screen luminance pulse is
  nauseating and repaints everything.
- Never use a `linear infinite` flicker with random-looking keyframes at 60 fps.

```css
/* Compliant CRT idle flicker: amplitude 3% of opacity on a low-alpha
   overlay, period 4s, so ~0.25 transitions/second — two orders of magnitude
   under the threshold. It reads as "the tube is alive", not as strobing.  */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) {
  .fx-crt {
    animation: fx-tube-idle 4s var(--ease-inout) infinite alternate;
  }
}
@keyframes fx-tube-idle {
  from { opacity: 0.97; }
  to   { opacity: 1; }
}
```

Note this animates `opacity` on an already-promoted fixed element: composite-only, no repaint.

If the user asks for a dramatic "power-on flash", make it a **one-shot** on the boot sequence
only, with a total of two transitions, never repeated, and skipped entirely under reduced
motion.

### 7.5 Text scramble / decode-on-reveal

The signature entrance for headings: the final text resolves out of random glyph noise. Done
correctly it is one `textContent` write per frame on one element and causes no layout shift,
because the string length is constant from the first frame.

```js
// theme/static/js/fx/scramble.js
import { subscribe } from '../core/scheduler.js';
import { reduced } from '../core/motion.js';

const NOISE = '!<>-_\\/[]{}—=+*^?#________ABCDEF0123456789';

/**
 * Resolve `el`'s text out of noise, one character at a time.
 * Invariants:
 *  - the element is sized by its FINAL text before the animation starts,
 *    so nothing reflows (we set a fixed min-width/min-height first);
 *  - only `textContent` is written — never innerHTML;
 *  - a screen reader reads the final text, never the noise, because we set
 *    aria-label to the final string and aria-hidden the animating node.
 */
export function scramble(el, { duration = 900, fps = 30 } = {}) {
  const final = el.textContent;
  if (!final) return () => {};

  // Accessibility: announce the real string, hide the churn.
  el.setAttribute('aria-label', final);
  const inner = document.createElement('span');
  inner.setAttribute('aria-hidden', 'true');
  inner.textContent = final;
  el.textContent = '';
  el.append(inner);

  if (reduced()) return () => {};      // final text already in place

  // Freeze the box so noise glyphs of different widths cannot shift layout.
  const rect = el.getBoundingClientRect();
  el.style.minWidth = `${Math.ceil(rect.width)}px`;
  el.style.minHeight = `${Math.ceil(rect.height)}px`;

  const n = final.length;
  // Each character gets a start and end time inside the total duration.
  const start = new Float32Array(n);
  const end = new Float32Array(n);
  for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
    start[i] = Math.random() * duration * 0.55;
    end[i] = start[i] + duration * (0.25 + Math.random() * 0.35);
  }

  let t0 = 0;
  const buf = new Array(n);

  const handle = subscribe((now) => {
    if (!t0) t0 = now;
    const t = now - t0;
    let done = true;
    for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
      const ch = final[i];
      if (ch === ' ' || t >= end[i]) { buf[i] = ch; continue; }
      done = false;
      buf[i] = t < start[i] ? ' ' : NOISE[(Math.random() * NOISE.length) | 0];
    }
    inner.textContent = buf.join('');
    if (done) {
      inner.textContent = final;
      el.style.minWidth = '';
      el.style.minHeight = '';
      handle.stop();
    }
  }, { fps, priority: 1 });

  return () => { handle.stop(); inner.textContent = final; };
}
```

**Cost:** one string join and one `textContent` assignment per frame, at 30 fps, for one
element. The `textContent` write invalidates layout for that element only — which is why the box
is frozen first. For a 30-character heading this is microseconds. **Do not run it on more than
one element at a time**, and never on body copy.

### 7.6 Typewriter without layout shift

The `steps()` + `width` trick is the wrong one: it animates `width`, which is layout, and it
only works for one line of monospace with a known character count.

The correct approach reserves the full box and reveals characters with a clip:

```css
/* Reserve the final size with an invisible copy, then reveal the visible
   copy left-to-right with a transform on an overlay mask.                 */
.fx-type { position: relative; display: inline-block; }
.fx-type__ghost { visibility: hidden; }             /* holds the box open  */
.fx-type__real  { position: absolute; inset: 0; white-space: pre-wrap; }
.fx-type__mask {
  position: absolute; inset: 0;
  background: var(--bg-page);
  transform-origin: right center;
  will-change: transform;
}
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) {
  .fx-type[data-run] .fx-type__mask {
    animation: fx-type-reveal var(--type-dur, 1.4s) steps(var(--type-steps, 24)) forwards;
  }
}
@keyframes fx-type-reveal { to { transform: scaleX(0); } }
```

The mask is a composite-only `scaleX`. The `steps()` timing gives the mechanical character-by-
character feel. The ghost copy guarantees the final layout exists from frame zero, so CLS is 0.

Limitation to be honest about: the mask is a solid colour, so it only works over a flat
background. Over the rain it would occlude it. In that case use the JS approach — append
characters to a `textContent` while the ghost holds the box — which is the same no-layout-shift
principle with a different mechanism.

**Do not use typewriter on:** anything the user needs to read now (it is a *delay* by
definition), any block longer than ~80 characters, or more than one element per page.

### 7.7 Effect cost summary

| Effect | Per-frame cost | Paint area | Safe count/page | Reduced-motion behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Matrix rain (canvas) | ~1 full-canvas fill + ~300 blits, 30 fps | Its own canvas | 1 | Static frame |
| Scanline overlay | 0 (static) or opacity-only | Full viewport, composite only | 1 | Opacity halved, no animation |
| RGB glitch on hover | 2 text rasters + clip repaint, 420 ms one-shot | The heading box | ≤ 3 | Not applied at all |
| Scramble decode | 1 `textContent` write, 30 fps, ~900 ms | One element | 1 at a time | Final text immediately |
| Typewriter (mask) | 1 composite transform | One element | 1 | Text fully visible |
| Tube idle flicker | opacity on promoted layer | 0 repaint | 1 | Disabled |
| Card hover glow | opacity on pseudo | The card | unlimited | Instant, no transition |

## 8. The boot sequence

A first-visit-only terminal boot intro. This is the highest-risk feature on the site because it
sits directly between the visitor and the content, so it is governed by one hard rule:

> **The boot sequence must never be the reason anybody waits.** Not the first visitor, not the
> returning visitor, not the reduced-motion user, not the screen-reader user, not the person on
> 3G. If it cannot satisfy that, it does not ship.

### 8.1 Architecture

The real page is **fully rendered in the DOM behind the theatre**. The boot overlay is an
`aria-hidden`, `pointer-events`-bearing element on top. Consequences:

- LCP is unaffected in the sense that the real content has already painted underneath — but the
  overlay covers it, and the browser's LCP heuristics measure the *largest contentful paint*,
  which may become the overlay itself. Therefore the overlay must be **transparent to LCP**:
  keep it text-only, small in visual weight, and — critically — **fade it out within a bounded
  time**. Measure LCP before and after with the `PerformanceObserver` in §17.4. If LCP regresses,
  the boot sequence loses.
- Nothing in the boot path is render-blocking. The module is `type="module" defer`; the overlay
  markup ships in the HTML with `hidden` and is unhidden by the module.
- Total duration is capped at **1600 ms**, hard.

### 8.2 Markup

```html
<!-- theme/partials/boot.html — emitted on every page, cheap when unused -->
<div class="boot" id="boot" hidden aria-hidden="true" data-boot>
  <pre class="boot__log" data-boot-log></pre>
  <p class="boot__hint" data-boot-hint></p>
</div>
```

Copy is emitted by the build in the current language, so JS ships no strings:

```html
<script type="application/json" id="boot-data" data-i18n>
{
  "lines": [
    "CzrXplo1t // research terminal",
    "kernel  ... ok",
    "crypto  ... ok",
    "net     ... ok",
    "index   ... 128 documentos",
    "listo."
  ],
  "hint": "pulsa cualquier tecla para saltar"
}
</script>
```

`<script type="application/json">` is inert: the CSP `script-src` directive does not execute it
because its type is not a JavaScript MIME type, and no engine will run it. This is the standard
CSP-safe way to ship data with a page. **Do not** use `type="text/template"` with HTML inside
it and then `innerHTML` it — that is how you reintroduce an injection sink.

### 8.3 Implementation

```js
// theme/static/js/fx/boot.js
import { reduced } from '../core/motion.js';

const KEY = 'czr.boot.seen';
const MAX_MS = 1600;

export function initBoot(root) {
  if (!root) return;

  // 1. Returning visitor within the session: never show it. Ever.
  let seen = false;
  try { seen = sessionStorage.getItem(KEY) === '1'; } catch { seen = true; }
  //    ^ private mode / storage disabled throws. Failing CLOSED (seen = true)
  //      is correct: when in doubt, do not block the reader.
  if (seen) { root.remove(); return; }

  // 2. Reduced motion: no theatre at all. The content is already there.
  if (reduced()) { markSeen(); root.remove(); return; }

  // 3. Slow connection or low-power: skip. The boot is a luxury.
  if (navigator.connection?.saveData || /2g/.test(navigator.connection?.effectiveType || '')) {
    markSeen(); root.remove(); return;
  }

  const dataEl = document.getElementById('boot-data');
  let data;
  try { data = JSON.parse(dataEl?.textContent || '{}'); } catch { data = {}; }
  const lines = Array.isArray(data.lines) ? data.lines : [];
  if (!lines.length) { markSeen(); root.remove(); return; }

  const log = root.querySelector('[data-boot-log]');
  const hint = root.querySelector('[data-boot-hint]');
  hint.textContent = data.hint || '';
  root.hidden = false;
  document.documentElement.classList.add('is-booting');

  // Per-line delay derived from the cap, so more lines never means longer.
  const per = Math.min(180, Math.floor(MAX_MS * 0.75 / lines.length));
  let i = 0;
  let timer = 0;
  let finished = false;

  function step() {
    if (finished) return;
    if (i >= lines.length) { finish(); return; }
    log.append(document.createTextNode(lines[i] + '\n'));  // textContent only
    i++;
    timer = setTimeout(step, per);
  }

  function finish() {
    if (finished) return;
    finished = true;
    clearTimeout(timer);
    markSeen();
    document.documentElement.classList.remove('is-booting');
    root.classList.add('is-leaving');
    // Wait for the fade, but never longer than the transition we defined.
    const done = () => { root.remove(); cleanup(); };
    root.addEventListener('transitionend', done, { once: true });
    setTimeout(done, 400);   // belt and braces: transitionend can be missed
  }

  function onSkip() { finish(); }
  function cleanup() {
    window.removeEventListener('keydown', onSkip, true);
    window.removeEventListener('pointerdown', onSkip, true);
    window.removeEventListener('wheel', onSkip, true);
    window.removeEventListener('touchstart', onSkip, true);
    clearTimeout(hardStop);
  }
  function markSeen() { try { sessionStorage.setItem(KEY, '1'); } catch {} }

  // Any input at all dismisses it. `capture: true` so nothing can swallow it.
  window.addEventListener('keydown', onSkip, { capture: true });
  window.addEventListener('pointerdown', onSkip, { capture: true });
  window.addEventListener('wheel', onSkip, { capture: true, passive: true });
  window.addEventListener('touchstart', onSkip, { capture: true, passive: true });

  // Absolute ceiling. If anything above misbehaves, this still ends it.
  const hardStop = setTimeout(finish, MAX_MS);

  step();
}

const el = document.querySelector('[data-boot]');
if (el) initBoot(el);
```

### 8.5 What it should say

Keep it short, plausible, and about *this site*. Six lines maximum. Real signal, no fake
progress bars, no `[####      ] 40%` that lies. Reference the site's actual state — the number
of indexed documents comes from the build, so it is true.

Never include: fake IP addresses, fake exploitation output, anything that reads as a real

This is a professional's blog, not a movie prop.

### 8.6 The four behaviours

| Condition | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| First visit, JS on, motion allowed | Boot plays, ≤ 1600 ms, skippable by any key/click/scroll/touch |
| Returning visit in the same session | `sessionStorage` hit → element removed before it is ever unhidden. Zero delay, zero paint |

| JS disabled | `hidden` attribute is never removed → never displayed. The real content is what the user sees, immediately |

show no boot at all. Open a new tab (new session) — it must show. Toggle reduced motion in
DevTools → Rendering → "Emulate CSS prefers-reduced-motion" — it must not show.

## 9. The command palette

`Ctrl+K` / `Cmd+K` opens an overlay that searches posts, tags, pages and actions (theme switch,

### 9.1 Keyboard model

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `Ctrl+K` / `Cmd+K` | Open; if already open, focus and select the query |
| `/` | Open, when focus is not in a text field |
| `Escape` | Close, restore focus to the element that opened it |
| `↓` / `Ctrl+N` | Next result (wraps) |

### 9.2 Markup and dialog semantics

Use the native `<dialog>` element with `showModal()`. It gives you, for free and correctly: the
top layer (no z-index fights), an inert background (no manual `inert` juggling for the rest of
the page), `Escape` handling, and a `::backdrop`. What it does **not** give you is focus
restoration in every engine, or a focus trap that respects your own ordering — so you still
implement those.

```html
<dialog class="palette" data-palette aria-label="Buscar en el sitio">

    <label class="u-visually-hidden" for="palette-q">Buscar</label>
    <input id="palette-q" class="palette__input" type="text"

    </p>
  </form>

```

Notes on the ARIA that people get wrong:

- The **combobox is the input**, not a wrapper. `aria-expanded` lives on the input.
- Selection is communicated with `aria-activedescendant` pointing at the option's `id`. **Focus
  never leaves the input.** Arrow keys move `aria-activedescendant`, not DOM focus. This is what
  makes the pattern work with a screen reader while the user keeps typing.
- Each `<li>` needs `role="option"` and `aria-selected="true"` on exactly one.
- The result count goes in a **separate polite live region**, not in the listbox, and is
  debounced so a fast typist does not queue nine announcements.

### 9.3 Fuzzy matching, from scratch

The algorithm: subsequence matching with positional scoring. A query matches if its characters
appear in order in the target. The score rewards matches that a human would consider "obvious":
consecutive runs, matches at word boundaries, matches at the very start, and short targets.

```js
// theme/static/js/palette/fuzzy.js
// Subsequence fuzzy matcher with scoring. No dependencies.
//
// Returns null when there is no match, otherwise { score, positions }.
// `positions` is an array of matched indices in `target`, used for
// highlighting (see §9.5 — it is used to build DOM nodes, never HTML).

const SCORE_START        = 60;   // match at index 0 of the target
const SCORE_WORD_BOUND   = 45;   // match right after a separator
const SCORE_CAMEL        = 30;   // lower→upper transition
const SCORE_CONSECUTIVE  = 25;   // immediately follows the previous match
const PENALTY_GAP        = -3;   // per skipped character between matches
const PENALTY_LEAD       = -1;   // per character before the first match
const PENALTY_LEN        = -0.4; // per character of target length
const BONUS_EXACT_SUB    = 80;   // the query appears verbatim as a substring
const BONUS_FULL         = 120;  // the target equals the query

const SEPARATORS = new Set([' ', '-', '_', '/', '.', ':', ',', '(', '[']);

/** Cheap ASCII-ish lowercase + accent folding for ES. */
const FOLD = { 'á':'a','à':'a','ä':'a','â':'a','é':'e','è':'e','ë':'e','ê':'e',
               'í':'i','ì':'i','ï':'i','î':'i','ó':'o','ò':'o','ö':'o','ô':'o',
               'ú':'u','ù':'u','ü':'u','û':'u','ñ':'n','ç':'c' };
export function fold(s) {
  let out = '';
  for (const ch of s.toLowerCase()) out += FOLD[ch] || ch;
  return out;
}

export function fuzzy(query, target) {
  if (!query) return { score: 0, positions: [] };
  const q = fold(query);
  const t = fold(target);
  if (q.length > t.length) return null;

  // Fast rejection: every query character must exist in the target.
  // For short queries this is cheaper than running the scan and failing.
  let qi = 0;
  for (let i = 0; i < t.length && qi < q.length; i++) {
    if (t[i] === q[qi]) qi++;
  }
  if (qi < q.length) return null;

  // Greedy left-to-right walk with lookahead for a better start.
  const positions = [];
  let score = 0;
  let ti = 0;
  let prev = -2;

  for (let k = 0; k < q.length; k++) {
    const ch = q[k];
    // Prefer a position that is a word boundary over the first raw match.
    let found = -1, boundary = -1;
    for (let i = ti; i < t.length; i++) {
      if (t[i] !== ch) continue;
      if (found === -1) found = i;
      const before = i === 0 ? ' ' : t[i - 1];
      const isBoundary = i === 0 || SEPARATORS.has(before);
      const isCamel = i > 0 && target[i - 1] === target[i - 1].toLowerCase()
                            && target[i] === target[i].toUpperCase()
                            && target[i] !== target[i].toLowerCase();
      if (isBoundary || isCamel) { boundary = i; break; }
      // Do not scan the whole string looking for a boundary far away; a
      // boundary 20 chars later is not worth the gap penalty.
      if (i - found > 24) break;
    }
    const idx = boundary !== -1 ? boundary : found;
    if (idx === -1) return null;

    if (idx === 0) score += SCORE_START;
    else {
      const before = t[idx - 1];
      if (SEPARATORS.has(before)) score += SCORE_WORD_BOUND;
      else if (target[idx - 1] === target[idx - 1].toLowerCase()
               && target[idx] !== target[idx].toLowerCase()
               && target[idx] === target[idx].toUpperCase()) score += SCORE_CAMEL;
    }
    if (idx === prev + 1) score += SCORE_CONSECUTIVE;
    else if (prev >= 0) score += PENALTY_GAP * (idx - prev - 1);
    else score += PENALTY_LEAD * idx;

    positions.push(idx);
    prev = idx;
    ti = idx + 1;
  }

  score += PENALTY_LEN * t.length;
  const sub = t.indexOf(q);
  if (sub !== -1) {
    score += BONUS_EXACT_SUB;
    if (sub === 0) score += 30;
  }
  if (t === q) score += BONUS_FULL;

  return { score, positions };
}

/**
 * Rank a list of items. Each item supplies weighted fields; the best field
 * wins and its weight multiplies the score, so a title match beats a body
 * match even when the body match scores higher on its own.
 */
export function rank(query, items, fields) {
  const out = [];
  for (const item of items) {
    let best = null;
    for (const { key, weight } of fields) {
      const value = item[key];
      if (!value) continue;
      const m = fuzzy(query, String(value));
      if (!m) continue;
      const s = m.score * weight;
      if (!best || s > best.score) best = { score: s, positions: m.positions, key };
    }
    if (best) out.push({ item, ...best });
  }
  out.sort((a, b) => b.score - a.score || String(a.item.title).localeCompare(String(b.item.title)));
  return out;
}
```

Typical field weights for this site:

| Field | Weight | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `title` | 1.0 | What people search for |
| `tags` | 0.75 | Strong topical signal, short strings |
| `slug` | 0.6 | Often what a returning reader remembers |
| `summary` | 0.35 | Long, so it scores high by accident; damp it |
| `section` | 0.3 | Coarse |

### 9.5 The XSS trap in highlighting

This is the single most common vulnerability in hand-rolled command palettes:

```js
// ❌ NEVER. Even "our own content" is not a reason. A post title containing
//    < or an ampersand breaks it; a title from a future contributor,
//    a tag typed by the author, or a query string echoed into the result
//    turns it into stored/reflected XSS — and it defeats the entire CSP.
li.innerHTML = title.replace(re, '<mark>$1</mark>');
```

Note the extra sting: your CSP forbids inline scripts, so a naive attacker payload with
`<script>` fails — but `<img src=x onerror=…>` also fails (inline handler), while
`<iframe src="//evil">`, CSS-based exfiltration and clickjacking overlays do not necessarily.
CSP is a mitigation, not a licence to inject.

The correct implementation builds nodes and never parses a string as markup:

```js
// theme/static/js/palette/highlight.js
/**
 * Build a DocumentFragment where the characters at `positions` are wrapped
 * in <mark>. No HTML parsing anywhere; every character enters the DOM as a
 * text node, so no string can ever become markup.
 */
export function highlight(text, positions) {
  const frag = document.createDocumentFragment();
  if (!positions || !positions.length) {
    frag.append(document.createTextNode(text));
    return frag;
  }
  const set = new Set(positions);
  let buf = '';
  let inMark = false;

  const flush = () => {
    if (!buf) return;
    if (inMark) {
      const m = document.createElement('mark');
      m.textContent = buf;            // textContent, always
      frag.append(m);
    } else {
      frag.append(document.createTextNode(buf));
    }
    buf = '';
  };

  for (let i = 0; i < text.length; i++) {
    const hit = set.has(i);
    if (hit !== inMark) { flush(); inMark = hit; }
    buf += text[i];
  }
  flush();
  return frag;
}
```

The rule, stated once so you never have to think about it again:

> **In this codebase, `innerHTML`, `outerHTML`, `insertAdjacentHTML`, `document.write` and
> `DOMParser` on runtime strings are banned.** Every dynamic node is created with
> `createElement` and filled with `textContent`. Enforce it:
>
> ```bash
> grep -rnE "innerHTML|outerHTML|insertAdjacentHTML|document\.write" \
>   /home/kali/Desktop/CzrXplo1t/theme/static/js/
> ```
>
> Any hit is a defect. There is no approved exception.

### 9.6 Focus management

```js
// theme/static/js/palette/focus.js
/**
 * Focus trap for a modal. <dialog>.showModal() already makes the rest of the
 * document inert, so the trap here is mostly about Tab ordering inside the
 * dialog and about restoring focus reliably on close, which engines do not
 * all handle identically.
 */
const FOCUSABLE = [
  'a[href]', 'button:not([disabled])', 'input:not([disabled])',
  'select:not([disabled])', 'textarea:not([disabled])',
  '[tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])',
].join(',');

export function trapFocus(dialog) {
  const opener = document.activeElement;

  function onKeydown(e) {
    if (e.key !== 'Tab') return;
    const nodes = [...dialog.querySelectorAll(FOCUSABLE)]
      .filter(n => n.offsetParent !== null || n === document.activeElement);
    if (!nodes.length) { e.preventDefault(); return; }
    const first = nodes[0], last = nodes[nodes.length - 1];
    if (e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === first) { e.preventDefault(); last.focus(); }
    else if (!e.shiftKey && document.activeElement === last) { e.preventDefault(); first.focus(); }
  }

  dialog.addEventListener('keydown', onKeydown);

  return function release() {
    dialog.removeEventListener('keydown', onKeydown);
    // Restore focus — but only if the opener is still in the document and
    // still focusable. Otherwise fall back to <body> so focus is never lost
    // to the void (which sends screen readers back to the top of the page).
    if (opener && opener.isConnected && typeof opener.focus === 'function') {
      opener.focus({ preventScroll: true });
    } else {
      document.body.setAttribute('tabindex', '-1');
      document.body.focus({ preventScroll: true });
      document.body.removeAttribute('tabindex');
    }
  };
}
```

### 9.7 The palette controller

```js
// theme/static/js/palette/index.js
import { rank } from './fuzzy.js';
import { highlight } from './highlight.js';
import { trapFocus } from './focus.js';

const MAX_RESULTS = 12;          // DOM growth cap; see §10.7
const DEBOUNCE_MS = 90;          // one frame of typing latency, not more

export function initPalette(dialog) {
  const input  = dialog.querySelector('[data-palette-input]');
  const list   = dialog.querySelector('[data-palette-list]');
  const status = dialog.querySelector('[data-palette-status]');

  let items = null;
  let results = [];
  let active = -1;
  let release = null;
  let timer = 0;
  let statusTimer = 0;
  let indexPromise = null;

  const lang = document.documentElement.lang || 'es';
  const loadIndex = () => (indexPromise ??= fetch(`/search-index.${lang}.json`)
    .then(r => r.ok ? r.json() : [])
    .then(rows => rows.map(([title, url, tags, summary]) =>
      ({ title, url, tags: tags.split('|').join(' '), summary })))
    .catch(() => []));

  function render() {
    list.replaceChildren();                       // cheap, no innerHTML
    active = results.length ? 0 : -1;

    const frag = document.createDocumentFragment();
    results.slice(0, MAX_RESULTS).forEach((r, i) => {
      const li = document.createElement('li');
      li.id = `palette-opt-${i}`;
      li.className = 'palette__item';
      li.setAttribute('role', 'option');
      li.setAttribute('aria-selected', i === 0 ? 'true' : 'false');

      const a = document.createElement('a');
      a.className = 'palette__link';
      a.href = r.item.url;
      a.tabIndex = -1;                            // focus stays in the input
      a.append(highlight(r.item.title, r.key === 'title' ? r.positions : []));

      const meta = document.createElement('span');
      meta.className = 'palette__meta';
      meta.textContent = r.item.tags;             // textContent

      li.append(a, meta);
      frag.append(li);
    });
    list.append(frag);

    input.setAttribute('aria-expanded', results.length ? 'true' : 'false');
    input.setAttribute('aria-activedescendant', active >= 0 ? `palette-opt-${active}` : '');

    // Debounced live region: announcing on every keystroke is noise.
    clearTimeout(statusTimer);
    statusTimer = setTimeout(() => {
      const n = Math.min(results.length, MAX_RESULTS);
      status.textContent = n === 0
        ? 'Sin resultados'
        : `${n} resultado${n === 1 ? '' : 's'}`;
    }, 350);
  }

  function move(delta) {
    if (!results.length) return;
    const n = Math.min(results.length, MAX_RESULTS);
    active = (active + delta + n) % n;
    for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
      list.children[i].setAttribute('aria-selected', i === active ? 'true' : 'false');
    }
    input.setAttribute('aria-activedescendant', `palette-opt-${active}`);
    list.children[active].scrollIntoView({ block: 'nearest' });
  }

  async function search(q) {
    items ??= await loadIndex();
    results = q.trim()
      ? rank(q, items, [
          { key: 'title', weight: 1.0 },
          { key: 'tags', weight: 0.75 },
          { key: 'summary', weight: 0.35 },
        ])
      : items.slice(0, MAX_RESULTS).map(item => ({ item, score: 0, positions: [], key: 'title' }));
    render();
  }

  input.addEventListener('input', () => {
    clearTimeout(timer);
    timer = setTimeout(() => search(input.value), DEBOUNCE_MS);
  });

  input.addEventListener('keydown', (e) => {
    switch (e.key) {
      case 'ArrowDown': e.preventDefault(); move(1); break;
      case 'ArrowUp':   e.preventDefault(); move(-1); break;
      case 'Home':      e.preventDefault(); active = 0; move(0); break;
      case 'End':       e.preventDefault(); active = Math.min(results.length, MAX_RESULTS) - 1; move(0); break;
      case 'PageDown':  e.preventDefault(); move(8); break;
      case 'PageUp':    e.preventDefault(); move(-8); break;
      case 'n': case 'p':
        if (!e.ctrlKey) return;
        e.preventDefault(); move(e.key === 'n' ? 1 : -1); break;
      case 'Enter': {
        if (active < 0) return;
        e.preventDefault();
        const href = results[active].item.url;
        if (e.ctrlKey || e.metaKey) window.open(href, '_blank', 'noopener');
        else window.location.assign(href);
        break;
      }
    }
  });

  function open() {
    if (dialog.open) { input.select(); return; }
    dialog.showModal();
    release = trapFocus(dialog);
    input.value = '';
    input.focus();
    search('');
  }
  function close() {
    if (!dialog.open) return;
    dialog.close();
  }
  dialog.addEventListener('close', () => { release?.(); release = null; });

  // Click on the backdrop closes. <dialog> reports backdrop clicks as clicks
  // on the dialog itself, so compare against the content box.
  dialog.addEventListener('click', (e) => {
    const r = dialog.getBoundingClientRect();
    const outside = e.clientX < r.left || e.clientX > r.right ||
                    e.clientY < r.top  || e.clientY > r.bottom;
    if (outside) close();
  });

  function isTyping(t) {
    return t instanceof HTMLElement &&
      (t.isContentEditable || /^(INPUT|TEXTAREA|SELECT)$/.test(t.tagName) ||
       t.closest('[data-terminal]') !== null);
  }

  window.addEventListener('keydown', (e) => {
    if ((e.ctrlKey || e.metaKey) && e.key.toLowerCase() === 'k') {
      e.preventDefault();      // Firefox would focus the address bar
      open();
    } else if (e.key === '/' && !isTyping(e.target) && !dialog.open) {
      e.preventDefault();
      open();
    }
  });

  // Touch equivalent: a visible button in the header (see §15.5).
  document.querySelector('[data-palette-open]')?.addEventListener('click', open);
}
```

```js
// theme/static/js/term/vfs.js
/**
 * Build an in-memory tree from the flat search index. Nodes are plain
 * objects; directories have `children`, files have `body` (a string).
 * Nothing here is ever inserted as HTML.
 */
export function buildVFS(rows) {
  const root = dir('/');
  const posts = dir('posts');
  const tags = dir('tags');
  root.children.set('posts', posts);
  root.children.set('tags', tags);

  const tagMap = new Map();

  for (const { title, url, tags: t, summary, date } of rows) {
    const slug = url.replace(/\/+$/, '').split('/').pop() || 'post';
    const d = dir(slug);
    d.url = url;
    d.children.set('meta', file('meta',
      `title:   ${title}\n` +
      `url:     ${url}\n` +
      `date:    ${date || 'n/a'}\n` +
      `tags:    ${t}\n`));
    d.children.set('summary', file('summary', summary || '(sin resumen)'));
    posts.children.set(slug, d);

    for (const tag of String(t).split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean)) {
      if (!tagMap.has(tag)) {
        const td = dir(tag);
        tagMap.set(tag, td);
        tags.children.set(tag, td);
      }
      const td = tagMap.get(tag);
      td.children.set(slug, file(slug, `${title}\n${url}\n`));
    }
  }

  root.children.set('about', file('about', 'CzrXplo1t — pentester. `cd /posts` para empezar.'));
  return root;
}

const dir  = (name) => ({ name, type: 'dir', children: new Map() });
const file = (name, body) => ({ name, type: 'file', body });

/** Resolve a path string against a cwd array of node names. Returns
 *  { node, path } or null. Handles `.`, `..`, absolute and relative. */
export function resolve(root, cwd, input) {
  const parts = (input.startsWith('/') ? input : cwd.join('/') + '/' + input)
    .split('/').filter(Boolean);
  const stack = [];
  let node = root;
  for (const p of parts) {
    if (p === '.') continue;
    if (p === '..') { stack.pop(); node = walk(root, stack); continue; }
    if (node.type !== 'dir') return null;
    const next = node.children.get(p);
    if (!next) return null;
    node = next;
    stack.push(p);
  }
  return { node, path: stack };
}

function walk(root, stack) {
  let n = root;
  for (const p of stack) n = n.children.get(p);
  return n;
}
```

### 10.3 Parsing with quoting

```js
// theme/static/js/term/parse.js
/**
 * Split a command line into argv, honouring single and double quotes and
 * backslash escapes. Returns null on an unterminated quote so the caller can
 * print a proper error instead of guessing.
 */
export function tokenize(line) {
  const out = [];
  let cur = '';
  let quote = null;
  let has = false;

  for (let i = 0; i < line.length; i++) {
    const c = line[i];
    if (quote) {
      if (c === '\\' && quote === '"' && i + 1 < line.length) { cur += line[++i]; continue; }
      if (c === quote) { quote = null; continue; }
      cur += c;
      continue;
    }
    if (c === '"' || c === "'") { quote = c; has = true; continue; }
    if (c === '\\' && i + 1 < line.length) { cur += line[++i]; has = true; continue; }
    if (/\s/.test(c)) {
      if (cur || has) { out.push(cur); cur = ''; has = false; }
      continue;
    }
    cur += c;
    has = true;
  }
  if (quote) return null;                 // unterminated
  if (cur || has) out.push(cur);
  return out;
}

/** Split argv into flags and positional arguments. */
export function parseArgs(argv) {
  const flags = new Set();
  const opts = new Map();
  const args = [];
  for (let i = 0; i < argv.length; i++) {
    const a = argv[i];
    if (a === '--') { args.push(...argv.slice(i + 1)); break; }
    if (a.startsWith('--')) {
      const [k, v] = a.slice(2).split('=');
      if (v !== undefined) opts.set(k, v); else flags.add(k);
    } else if (a.startsWith('-') && a.length > 1) {
      for (const ch of a.slice(1)) flags.add(ch);
    } else args.push(a);
  }
  return { flags, opts, args };
}

/** Convert a glob to a RegExp. Escapes everything else — no ReDoS surface. */
export function globToRe(glob, flags = '') {
  const esc = glob.replace(/[.+^${}()|[\]\\]/g, '\\$&')
                  .replace(/\*/g, '.*')
                  .replace(/\?/g, '.');
  return new RegExp(`^${esc}$`, flags);
}
```

`globToRe` escapes every regex metacharacter *except* the two it translates. That matters: a
user typing `find (((((a+)+)+)$` must not be able to build a catastrophically backtracking
pattern. There is no user-supplied regex anywhere in this terminal; `grep` uses
`String.prototype.includes` on folded strings, not `RegExp`.

### 10.4 Output: `textContent` only, with a hard cap

```js
// theme/static/js/term/io.js
const MAX_LINES = 500;      // hard DOM cap; older lines are dropped
const MAX_LINE_LEN = 2000;  // truncate pathological single lines

export function createIO(outputEl) {
  let count = 0;

  /** Append one line. `cls` selects a colour token via a CSS class. */
  function write(text, cls) {
    const line = document.createElement('div');
    line.className = cls ? `term__line term__line--${cls}` : 'term__line';
    const s = String(text);
    line.textContent = s.length > MAX_LINE_LEN ? s.slice(0, MAX_LINE_LEN) + ' …[truncado]' : s;
    outputEl.append(line);
    count++;
    // Unbounded output is a memory leak and a scroll-performance disaster.
    while (count > MAX_LINES && outputEl.firstChild) {
      outputEl.firstChild.remove();
      count--;
    }
    return line;
  }

  function writeBlock(text, cls) {
    for (const l of String(text).split('\n')) write(l, cls);
  }

  /** Echo the command the user typed, with the prompt, as history. */
  function echo(prompt, cmd) {
    const line = document.createElement('div');
    line.className = 'term__line term__line--echo';
    const p = document.createElement('span');
    p.className = 'term__prompt';
    p.textContent = prompt;
    const c = document.createElement('span');
    c.textContent = ' ' + cmd;          // textContent: user input, always
    line.append(p, c);
    outputEl.append(line);
    count++;
    return line;
  }

  function clear() { outputEl.replaceChildren(); count = 0; }

  function scrollToEnd() {
    // scrollTop assignment is a layout write; batch it after the writes.
    outputEl.scrollTop = outputEl.scrollHeight;
  }

  return { write, writeBlock, echo, clear, scrollToEnd, get count() { return count; } };
}
```

```js
  // ---- execution --------------------------------------------------------
  async function exec(line) {
    const argv = tokenize(line);
    if (argv === null) return io.write('error de sintaxis: comilla sin cerrar', 'err');
    if (!argv.length) return;

    // The rm easter egg needs the raw line, so it is handled before dispatch.
    if (/^rm\s+-rf\s+\/\s*$/.test(line.trim())) {
      io.write('rm: no voy a hacer eso.', 'err');
      io.write('nice try.', 'muted');
      return;
    }

    const name = argv[0];
    const cmd = commands[name];
    if (!cmd) {
      io.write(`${name}: comando no encontrado`, 'err');
      // Suggest the nearest command by simple edit distance — cheap and kind.
      const near = Object.keys(commands)
        .map(n => [n, editDistance(n, name)])
        .filter(([, d]) => d <= 2)
        .sort((a, b) => a[1] - b[1])[0];
      if (near) io.write(`¿querías decir "${near[0]}"?`, 'muted');
      return;
    }
    try {
      await cmd.run(parseArgs(argv.slice(1)));
    } catch (err) {
      io.write(`${name}: error interno`, 'err');
      console.error(err);
    }
  }

  function editDistance(a, b) {
    const m = a.length, n = b.length;
    let prev = Array.from({ length: n + 1 }, (_, i) => i);
    for (let i = 1; i <= m; i++) {
      const cur = [i];
      for (let j = 1; j <= n; j++) {
        cur[j] = Math.min(prev[j] + 1, cur[j - 1] + 1,
                          prev[j - 1] + (a[i - 1] === b[j - 1] ? 0 : 1));
      }
      prev = cur;
    }
    return prev[n];
  }

  // ---- input handling ---------------------------------------------------
  async function submit() {
    const line = inputEl.value;
    io.echo(prompt(), line);
    inputEl.value = '';
    completions = []; completionIdx = -1;
    if (line.trim()) {
      history.push(line);
      if (history.length > HIST_MAX) history = history.slice(-HIST_MAX);
      try { localStorage.setItem(HIST_KEY, JSON.stringify(history)); } catch {}
    }
    histIdx = history.length;
    await exec(line);
    io.scrollToEnd();
  }

  async function complete() {
    const value = inputEl.value;
    const parts = value.split(/\s+/);
    const isFirst = parts.length === 1;

    if (completions.length) {                 // cycle through existing matches
      completionIdx = (completionIdx + 1) % completions.length;
      parts[parts.length - 1] = completions[completionIdx];
      inputEl.value = parts.join(' ');
      return;
    }

    const frag = parts[parts.length - 1] || '';
    if (isFirst) {
      completions = Object.keys(commands).filter(n => n.startsWith(frag)).sort();
    } else {
      const r = resolve(await ensureVFS(), cwd, frag.includes('/')
        ? frag.slice(0, frag.lastIndexOf('/') + 1) : '.');
      const base = frag.includes('/') ? frag.slice(frag.lastIndexOf('/') + 1) : frag;
      const dirPrefix = frag.includes('/') ? frag.slice(0, frag.lastIndexOf('/') + 1) : '';
      completions = r && r.node.type === 'dir'
        ? [...r.node.children.keys()].filter(k => k.startsWith(base)).sort()
            .map(k => dirPrefix + k)
        : [];
    }
    if (!completions.length) return;
    if (completions.length === 1) {
      parts[parts.length - 1] = completions[0];
      inputEl.value = parts.join(' ');
      completions = [];
      return;
    }
    // Multiple: complete to the longest common prefix, then list.
    const lcp = completions.reduce((a, b) => {
      let i = 0; while (i < a.length && i < b.length && a[i] === b[i]) i++;
      return a.slice(0, i);
    });
    if (lcp.length > frag.length) {
      parts[parts.length - 1] = lcp;
      inputEl.value = parts.join(' ');
      completions = [];
      return;
    }
    io.echo(prompt(), value);
    io.write(completions.join('  '), 'muted');
    io.scrollToEnd();
    completionIdx = -1;
  }

  inputEl.addEventListener('keydown', (e) => {
    switch (e.key) {
      case 'Enter': e.preventDefault(); submit(); break;
      case 'Tab': e.preventDefault(); complete(); break;
      case 'ArrowUp':
        e.preventDefault();
        if (histIdx === history.length) draft = inputEl.value;
        if (histIdx > 0) inputEl.value = history[--histIdx];
        moveCaretToEnd(inputEl);
        break;
      case 'ArrowDown':
        e.preventDefault();
        if (histIdx < history.length - 1) inputEl.value = history[++histIdx];
        else { histIdx = history.length; inputEl.value = draft; }
        moveCaretToEnd(inputEl);
        break;
      case 'l': if (e.ctrlKey) { e.preventDefault(); io.clear(); } break;
      case 'c': if (e.ctrlKey && !inputEl.selectionStart) {
                  e.preventDefault(); io.echo(prompt(), inputEl.value + '^C'); inputEl.value = '';
                } break;
      case 'u': if (e.ctrlKey) { e.preventDefault(); inputEl.value = ''; } break;
      default:
        if (e.key !== 'Tab') { completions = []; completionIdx = -1; }
    }
  });

  function moveCaretToEnd(el) {
    requestAnimationFrame(() => { el.selectionStart = el.selectionEnd = el.value.length; });
  }

  // Clicking anywhere in the terminal focuses the input — but not when the
  // user is selecting text to copy, which is the whole point of a terminal.
  root.addEventListener('mouseup', () => {
    if (!String(window.getSelection())) inputEl.focus();
  });

  syncPrompt();
  io.write('escribe `help` para empezar.', 'muted');
}

const term = document.querySelector('[data-terminal]');
if (term) initTerminal(term);
```

### 10.6 Markup, accessibility and mobile

```html
<section class="term" data-terminal aria-label="Terminal interactiva del sitio">
  <div class="term__output" data-term-output role="log" aria-live="polite" aria-atomic="false"
       tabindex="0"></div>
  <div class="term__inputrow">
    <span class="term__prompt" data-term-prompt aria-hidden="true">czr@xplo1t:/$</span>
    <label class="u-visually-hidden" for="term-input">Comando</label>
    <input class="term__input" id="term-input" data-term-input type="text"
           autocomplete="off" autocorrect="off" autocapitalize="none" spellcheck="false"
           enterkeyhint="go" inputmode="text">
  </div>
</section>
```

Accessibility specifics:

- `role="log"` + `aria-live="polite"` announces new output without interrupting. **Not
  `assertive`** — a terminal that interrupts is unusable with a screen reader.
- `aria-atomic="false"` so only the new lines are read, not the whole buffer.
- The output region is `tabindex="0"` so a keyboard user can scroll it. It must have a visible
  focus indicator.
- The prompt span is `aria-hidden` because the input's label already says "Comando"; otherwise
  the prompt string is read before every interaction.
- Long output floods the live region. Mitigation: when a command emits more than ~20 lines,
  temporarily set `aria-live="off"` on the log, write the output, then announce a summary
  ("`ls`: 34 entradas") through a separate polite status element and restore `aria-live`.

Mobile specifics:

- `autocapitalize="none"`, `autocorrect="off"`, `spellcheck="false"` — otherwise iOS capitalises
  every command and "corrects" `ls` to "is".
- `enterkeyhint="go"` labels the virtual return key.
- The on-screen keyboard covers the bottom of the viewport. Use `scrollIntoView({ block:
  'nearest' })` on focus and size the terminal with `dvh`, not `vh`:

```css
.term { max-block-size: min(60dvh, 520px); }
.term__output { overflow-y: auto; overscroll-behavior: contain; }
```

- There is no `Tab` key and no arrow keys on a phone. Provide a small toolbar of tap buttons
  above the input for `Tab`, `↑`, `↓`, `Ctrl+C` and `help` when `@media (pointer: coarse)`. Each
  button is ≥ 44 × 44 CSS px and has an accessible name.

### 10.7 DOM growth and memory

Unbounded output is the failure mode of every web terminal. Three caps, all enforced above:

| Cap | Value | Enforced in |
|---|---|---|
| Lines retained in the DOM | 500 | `io.write` trims from the front |
| Characters per line | 2000 | `io.write` truncates |
| Results per traversal command | 200 (`find`), 100 (`grep`), 300 (`tree`) | Each command |
| History entries | 100 | `submit()` |

Verify by pasting a `tree -L 9` on a large index and counting nodes:

```js
document.querySelectorAll('[data-term-output] > *').length   // must stay <= 500
```

### 10.8 The output-safety rule

> Everything the terminal prints is created with `document.createElement` and filled with
> `textContent`. A post title containing `<`, a tag containing `&`, or a user typing
> `echo "<img src=x>"` produces **visible text**, never a node. There is no code path in which a
> string reaches the DOM as markup.

This is not paranoia about a static site: it is the property that makes the terminal safe to
extend later, and it is the property that keeps the CSP meaningful.

## 11. Media and GIF strategy

### 11.1 The honest engineering position

Animated GIF is a 1987 codec. It is limited to 256 colours per frame, has no inter-frame motion
compensation worth the name, no modern entropy coding, and cannot be hardware-decoded. A
screen-capture of a terminal session — which is what most of this site's animations are — is the
worst case for GIF's palette and the best case for a real video codec, because consecutive
frames are almost identical.

Real numbers you should expect, and must verify per asset rather than quote from memory: a
20-second 1280×720 terminal capture is typically **4–8 MB as GIF**, **150–500 KB as VP9 WebM**,
**200–600 KB as H.264 MP4**, and **300 KB–1 MB as animated WebP**. That is an order of
magnitude, and it is paid by every visitor on every load.

Beyond bytes, GIF is bad in three more ways that matter here:

1. **It cannot be paused.** A GIF starts immediately, loops forever, and the only way to stop it
   is to remove it from the document. That violates WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) for
   any animation longer than five seconds.
2. **It decodes on the main thread.** A large GIF competes with your JavaScript for the same
   frame budget. `<video>` decodes off-thread, usually in hardware.
3. **It has no poster.** The first frame paints only after enough of the file has arrived.

### 11.2 The hierarchy — choose the highest applicable option

| Rank | Technique | Use when | Typical size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CSS animation / transition** | The motion is geometric: a cursor blink, a bar filling, a panel sliding, a glow pulse | 0 bytes over the network |
| 2 | **Inline SVG + CSS (or SMIL)** | Diagrams, logos, line art, animated flow charts, network topologies | 1–15 KB, scales perfectly, themeable with `currentColor` |
| 3 | **Animated WebP / AVIF** | Short loops with transparency, or where a `<video>` element is overkill | 5–20 % of the GIF |
| 4 | **`<video muted playsinline loop preload="none" poster>`** | Screen captures, terminal sessions, anything > 3 seconds | 5–15 % of the GIF |
| 5 | **True GIF** | Only when the *source itself* is a GIF you cannot re-encode, or it is genuinely tiny (< 100 KB) | — |

Rule: an animation that can be expressed as option 1 or 2 must never be shipped as options 3–5.
A "loading" spinner as a GIF is a defect. A terminal cursor as a GIF is a defect.

### 11.3 `ffmpeg` conversions

**First, probe. Never claim a conversion you did not run.**

```bash
if command -v ffmpeg >/dev/null 2>&1; then
  ffmpeg -version | head -1
else
  echo "FFMPEG_MISSING"
fi
```

If it prints `FFMPEG_MISSING`, you **stop**, you **do not** silently keep the GIF and call it
done, and you **do not** report a conversion. You tell the user, in Spanish, exactly this:

> `ffmpeg` no está instalado, así que no he podido convertir `X.gif`. Instálalo con
> `sudo apt update && sudo apt install ffmpeg` (Kali/Debian) y vuelve a lanzarme, o dime que
> deje el GIF tal cual asumiendo su coste de N MB.

Then leave the asset alone and record it in the report as a pending item.

When it is available:

```bash
IN=theme/static/media/gif/demo.gif
BASE=theme/static/media/demo

# --- 1. Inspect the source first. Never convert blind. ---------------------
ffprobe -v error -select_streams v:0 \
  -show_entries stream=width,height,nb_frames,avg_frame_rate,duration \
  -of default=noprint_wrappers=1 "$IN"
ls -l "$IN"

# --- 2. VP9 WebM. Best compression; the primary source. --------------------
#   -crf 32       quality target (lower = better; 28-36 is the useful range)
#   -b:v 0        constant-quality mode; without this -crf is ignored for VP9
#   -row-mt 1     row-based multithreading
#   -deadline good / -cpu-used 2   quality/speed trade-off
#   scale: force even dimensions; many encoders reject odd sizes
#   fps=24        terminal captures do not need 50fps
ffmpeg -y -i "$IN" \
  -vf "fps=24,scale=trunc(iw/2)*2:trunc(ih/2)*2:flags=lanczos" \
  -c:v libvpx-vp9 -crf 32 -b:v 0 -row-mt 1 -deadline good -cpu-used 2 \
  -pix_fmt yuv420p -an "$BASE.webm"

# --- 3. H.264 MP4 fallback (Safari, older engines). ------------------------
#   -profile:v main -level 4.0  broad device support
#   -movflags +faststart        moov atom first, so it starts before full load
#   yuv420p is MANDATORY for Safari; yuv444p will not play
ffmpeg -y -i "$IN" \
  -vf "fps=24,scale=trunc(iw/2)*2:trunc(ih/2)*2:flags=lanczos" \
  -c:v libx264 -crf 23 -preset slow -profile:v main -level 4.0 \
  -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart -an "$BASE.mp4"

# --- 4. Animated WebP (when transparency or a tiny loop is needed). --------
#   -lossless 0 -q:v 60 -compression_level 6 -loop 0
ffmpeg -y -i "$IN" \
  -vf "fps=20,scale=trunc(iw/2)*2:trunc(ih/2)*2:flags=lanczos" \
  -c:v libwebp -lossless 0 -q:v 60 -compression_level 6 -loop 0 \
  -preset picture -an "$BASE.webp"

# --- 5. Poster frame. Take a frame with content, not frame 0 (often blank). -
ffmpeg -y -i "$IN" -vf "select=eq(n\,12),scale=trunc(iw/2)*2:-2" \
  -frames:v 1 -q:v 2 "$BASE-poster.jpg"
# …and a modern one:
ffmpeg -y -i "$BASE-poster.jpg" -c:v libwebp -q:v 78 "$BASE-poster.webp"

# --- 6. Report the result. Numbers, not adjectives. ------------------------
ls -l "$IN" "$BASE.webm" "$BASE.mp4" "$BASE.webp" "$BASE-poster.webp" \
  | awk '{printf "%-52s %10.1f KB\n", $9, $5/1024}'
```

Two extra flags worth knowing:

- If the source has a transparent background, VP9 supports alpha via
  `-pix_fmt yuva420p -auto-alt-ref 0`; H.264 does not, so use animated WebP instead.
- For very long captures, add `-g 48` (keyframe interval) so seeking is responsive.

### 11.4 Click-to-play markup

Progressive enhancement, keyboard accessible, labelled, no autoplay, no layout shift.

```html
<figure class="media" data-media>
  <div class="media__frame" style="aspect-ratio: 1280 / 720">
    <video class="media__video" data-media-video
           width="1280" height="720"
           poster="/static/media/demo-poster.webp"
           preload="none" muted loop playsinline disablepictureinpicture>
      <source src="/static/media/demo.webm" type="video/webm">
      <source src="/static/media/demo.mp4"  type="video/mp4">
      <!-- Last-resort fallback for engines with no <video> at all, and the
           honest text alternative. Never a 6 MB GIF here. -->
      <a href="/static/media/demo.mp4">Descargar el vídeo de la demostración (MP4)</a>
    </video>
    <button class="media__play" type="button" data-media-play
            aria-label="Reproducir: escalada de privilegios en el laboratorio">
      <span class="media__playicon" aria-hidden="true">▶</span>
    </button>
  </div>
  <figcaption class="media__caption" id="demo-cap">
    Escalada de privilegios mediante el <em>helper</em> vulnerable. 18 s, sin audio.
  </figcaption>

```

`aspect-ratio` on the frame plus `width`/`height` on the video means **zero layout shift** even
before the poster loads. `preload="none"` means the video bytes are not fetched at all until the
user asks — that is what keeps a page with six demos fast.

```js
// theme/static/js/media/clicktoplay.js
import { reduced } from '../core/motion.js';

for (const fig of document.querySelectorAll('[data-media]')) {
  const video = fig.querySelector('[data-media-video]');
  const btn = fig.querySelector('[data-media-play]');
  if (!video || !btn) continue;

  const label = btn.getAttribute('aria-label') || 'Vídeo';

  function play() {
    video.preload = 'auto';
    const p = video.play();
    if (p && p.catch) p.catch(() => { /* autoplay policy or codec: stay paused */ });
  }
  function pause() { video.pause(); }

  btn.addEventListener('click', () => (video.paused ? play() : pause()));

  video.addEventListener('play', () => {
    fig.dataset.playing = '';
    btn.setAttribute('aria-label', label.replace(/^Reproducir/, 'Pausar'));
    btn.setAttribute('aria-pressed', 'true');
  });
  video.addEventListener('pause', () => {
    delete fig.dataset.playing;
    btn.setAttribute('aria-label', label.replace(/^Pausar/, 'Reproducir'));
    btn.setAttribute('aria-pressed', 'false');
  });

  // Clicking the video itself toggles, matching every video player ever.
  video.addEventListener('click', () => (video.paused ? play() : pause()));

  // Stop when it scrolls away: an unseen loop is wasted decode and battery.
  if ('IntersectionObserver' in window) {
    new IntersectionObserver(([e]) => { if (!e.isIntersecting) pause(); },
      { threshold: 0 }).observe(video);
  }

  // Reduced motion: never start on its own, and remove `loop` so one play
  // ends rather than repeating forever.
  if (reduced()) video.loop = false;
}
```

### 11.5 The no-JS path

Without JS the button does nothing. That is unacceptable, so the markup must degrade. Two
options; pick per asset:

**Option A — native controls when JS is absent.** Ship the `<video>` with `controls` in the HTML
and have the module *remove* `controls` and add the custom button. No JS → native controls,
fully functional, keyboard accessible, free.

```js
// first lines of clicktoplay.js
video.removeAttribute('controls');
btn.hidden = false;              // the button ships with `hidden` in the HTML
```

```html
<video … controls preload="none" poster="…">…</video>
<button class="media__play" hidden data-media-play …>…</button>
```

This is the recommended default: it is the only version where the no-JS experience is *equal*,
not merely non-broken.

**Option B — `<details>` wrapper.** For heavy assets, wrap the media in
`<details><summary>Ver la demostración (1.2 MB)</summary>…</details>`. Zero JS, zero bytes until
opened by the browser's own lazy behaviour (note: `<details>` does not by itself prevent the
fetch for `img`, but `preload="none"` on `<video>` does).

### 11.7 Images that are not animated

- Always `width` and `height` attributes, or `aspect-ratio` in CSS. No exceptions.
- `loading="lazy"` on everything below the fold; **never** on the LCP image.
- `decoding="async"` everywhere.

## 12. Page transitions and perceived speed

> **Perceived speed comes from eliminating work, not from animating the wait.** A 120 ms
> cross-fade over a navigation that takes 400 ms makes it feel like 520 ms. Prefetching that
> same navigation makes it feel instant, and then the cross-fade is a nicety rather than a
> disguise.

### 12.1 View Transitions, with honest feature detection

```css
/* Cross-document view transitions: opt in, then define the animation.
   Everything here is inert in engines without support.                    */
@view-transition { navigation: auto; }

::view-transition-old(root) {
  animation: vt-out 90ms var(--ease-in) both;
}
::view-transition-new(root) {
  animation: vt-in 140ms var(--ease-out) both;
}
@keyframes vt-out { to { opacity: 0; } }
@keyframes vt-in  { from { opacity: 0; } }

/* Name the elements that should morph across pages. The post title in a
   card and the <h1> on the post page share a name, so the browser animates
   between them. Names must be UNIQUE per page — two elements with the same
   view-transition-name silently disable the whole transition.             */
.post-card__title { view-transition-name: var(--vt-title, none); }
.post__title      { view-transition-name: var(--vt-title, none); }

@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  ::view-transition-old(root),
  ::view-transition-new(root) { animation: none; }
}
```

Keep the durations **under 150 ms**. A view transition blocks the new page's interactivity for
its duration; a 400 ms "cinematic" transition is 400 ms of the user not being able to click.

Detection when you need to branch in JS:

```js
const supportsVT = typeof document.startViewTransition === 'function';
```

Never write code that requires it. The whole point is that its absence changes nothing.

### 12.2 Prefetch on intent

```js
// theme/static/js/nav/prefetch.js
// Prefetch same-origin document URLs on hover/focus/touch intent.
// Constraints: concurrency cap, one fetch per URL ever, opt-out on
// Save-Data and slow connections, and never on a link the user cannot use.

const MAX_CONCURRENT = 2;
const HOVER_DELAY = 65;          // ms of sustained intent before fetching
const seen = new Set();
let inflight = 0;

const conn = navigator.connection;
const disabled =
  conn?.saveData === true ||
  /2g/.test(conn?.effectiveType || '') ||
  matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-data: reduce)').matches;

function eligible(a) {
  if (!a || !a.href) return false;
  if (a.origin !== location.origin) return false;
  if (a.hasAttribute('download') || a.target === '_blank') return false;
  if (a.getAttribute('rel')?.includes('external')) return false;
  if (a.pathname === location.pathname) return false;
  if (seen.has(a.href)) return false;
  // Do not prefetch assets; only documents.
  if (/\.(zip|pdf|mp4|webm|gif|png|jpe?g|svg|json)$/i.test(a.pathname)) return false;
  return true;
}

function prefetch(url) {
  if (inflight >= MAX_CONCURRENT) return;
  seen.add(url);
  inflight++;
  // <link rel=prefetch> lets the browser prioritise it below everything the
  // current page still needs — better than fetch(), which competes.
  const link = document.createElement('link');
  link.rel = 'prefetch';
  link.as = 'document';
  link.href = url;
  link.addEventListener('load', () => { inflight--; }, { once: true });
  link.addEventListener('error', () => { inflight--; }, { once: true });
  document.head.append(link);
  // Safety valve: some engines fire neither event for prefetch.
  setTimeout(() => { if (inflight > 0) inflight--; }, 4000);
}

if (!disabled) {
  let timer = 0;
  document.addEventListener('pointerover', (e) => {
    const a = e.target.closest?.('a[href]');
    if (!eligible(a)) return;
    clearTimeout(timer);
    timer = setTimeout(() => prefetch(a.href), HOVER_DELAY);
  }, { passive: true });

  document.addEventListener('pointerout', () => clearTimeout(timer), { passive: true });

  // Keyboard users get the same benefit.
  document.addEventListener('focusin', (e) => {
    const a = e.target.closest?.('a[href]');
    if (eligible(a)) prefetch(a.href);
  }, { passive: true });

  // Touch: touchstart precedes the click by ~90-300ms. Free head start.
  document.addEventListener('touchstart', (e) => {
    const a = e.target.closest?.('a[href]');
    if (eligible(a)) prefetch(a.href);
  }, { passive: true });
}
```

### 12.3 `speculationrules`

Where supported, this is strictly better than manual prefetching: the browser does the intent
detection with real telemetry and can *prerender* (render the page in the background) rather
than merely prefetch.

```html
<script type="speculationrules">
{
  "prefetch": [{
    "where": { "and": [
      { "href_matches": "/*" },
      { "not": { "href_matches": "/*\\?*" } },
      { "not": { "selector_matches": "[rel~=nofollow]" } },
      { "not": { "selector_matches": ".no-prefetch" } }
    ]},
    "eagerness": "moderate"
  }],
  "prerender": [{
    "where": { "selector_matches": "a.post-card__link" },
    "eagerness": "conservative"
  }]
}
</script>
```

**CSP note, and this is a real trap:** `<script type="speculationrules">` is governed by
`script-src`. Under a strict policy it needs `'inline-speculation-rules'` in `script-src`, which
is a *narrow* keyword that permits only this type and does not enable inline JavaScript. Adding
it does **not** violate the "no `unsafe-inline`" invariant. Alternatively, serve the rules from
a separate file via a `Speculation-Rules: "/rules.json"` response header — but GitHub Pages does
not let you set headers, so on this site the inline form plus the CSP keyword is the only
option. Add it explicitly:

```
script-src 'self' 'inline-speculation-rules';
```

`eagerness` values, from most to least aggressive: `immediate`, `eager`, `moderate` (hover ~200
ms), `conservative` (pointerdown). Prerender is expensive — a full render of another page — so
keep it `conservative` and restricted to a selector, never `/*`.

If both `speculationrules` and the manual prefetcher are active, the manual one should stand
down to avoid double fetching:

```js
const hasSpecRules = HTMLScriptElement.supports?.('speculationrules');
if (hasSpecRules) { /* skip manual prefetch entirely */ }
```

### 12.4 Skeletons: do not

Skeleton screens exist because an app cannot render until data arrives. This site is static
HTML: the content **is** the first paint. A skeleton here would mean deliberately hiding content
in order to animate a placeholder over it. Never do that.

The equivalent discipline:

- No content is hidden and then revealed by JS on load. No `opacity: 0` + `.is-visible` reveal
  pattern on article content — with JS disabled or slow, the article is invisible. If you want
  a fade-in, gate it so the default state is *visible* and the animation only applies once JS
  has confirmed it can also remove it:

```css
/* Correct: visible by default; the class only exists once JS is running. */
:root.js .reveal { opacity: 0; transform: translateY(8px); }
:root.js .reveal.is-in { opacity: 1; transform: none;
  transition: opacity var(--dur-base) var(--ease-out),
              transform var(--dur-base) var(--ease-out); }
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  :root.js .reveal { opacity: 1; transform: none; }
}
```

- No spinner for anything that takes less than 300 ms; below that a spinner *adds* perceived
  latency by drawing attention to the wait.

### 12.5 Scroll restoration

```js
// Let the browser do it; it is better at it than you are.
if ('scrollRestoration' in history) history.scrollRestoration = 'auto';
```

The only reason to set `'manual'` is a single-page app, which this is not. What you must do
instead is **not break** the browser's restoration:

- Do not lazily insert content above the current scroll position after load.
- `content-visibility: auto` with `contain-intrinsic-size: auto <len>` (note the `auto`
  keyword) lets the browser remember real sizes, which keeps restoration accurate.
- `scroll-behavior: smooth` on `:root` is fine, but must be disabled under reduced motion (the
  global backstop in §5.6 already does this) and must not be applied when restoring.
- Anchor targets need `scroll-margin-block-start` equal to the sticky header height, or the
  heading lands under the header:

```css
:where(h1, h2, h3, h4, [id]) { scroll-margin-block-start: calc(var(--header-h, 3.5rem) + var(--space-4)); }
```

## 13. Theming

### 13.1 The model

```
initial signal:  prefers-color-scheme  (pure CSS, works with JS disabled)
explicit choice: data-theme on <html>  (set by JS, persisted in localStorage)
priority:        explicit choice > OS signal > default (green)
```

Four schemes: `green` (default), `amber`, `ice`, `redteam`. All four are dark; there is no light
theme, and that is a deliberate product decision — a light CRT phosphor theme is a contradiction.
`prefers-color-scheme: light` therefore does **not** switch to a light theme; it selects the
highest-legibility dark scheme (`ice`), which is the closest thing to "I want something calmer".
Document this so nobody "fixes" it later.

```css
/* Default: green. */
:root { /* …green tokens… */ }

/* OS signal, only when the user has not chosen explicitly. :root:not([data-theme])
   is the "no explicit choice" state, so the media query cannot fight the toggle. */
@media (prefers-color-scheme: light) {
  :root:not([data-theme]) { /* …ice tokens… */ }
}
```

### 13.2 The flash-of-wrong-theme problem under a strict CSP

The standard fix is a tiny **inline** script in `<head>` that reads `localStorage` and sets
`data-theme` before first paint. **That is forbidden here**, because `script-src` has no
`unsafe-inline`. So solve it properly. Five options, evaluated:

| Option | Mechanism | Flash? | CSP-legal? | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. External blocking script in `<head>`** | `<script src="/js/theme-boot.js"></script>` — no `defer`, no `type=module`, so it executes before the parser continues and before first paint | **No flash** | **Yes** — `script-src 'self'` permits external same-origin scripts; only *inline* is blocked | One extra request (~350 B, same origin, HTTP/2 multiplexed, cacheable forever with a hashed name); blocks parsing for the duration of that request | **Recommended.** Preload it with `<link rel="preload" as="script">` as the very first element in `<head>` |
| **B. CSP hash for an inline script** | `script-src 'self' 'sha256-…'` — a hash allowlists one exact inline script *without* `unsafe-inline` | **No flash** | **Yes**, strictly speaking: hashes are the CSP-sanctioned way to allow specific inline code | `build.py` must compute the SHA-256 of the exact script text and inject it into the `<meta http-equiv="Content-Security-Policy">` on every page. Fragile: one whitespace change and every page breaks silently | **Acceptable second choice** if the build owns it end-to-end and there is a test asserting the hash matches |
| **C. No JS at all; `prefers-color-scheme` only** | Pure CSS | No flash | Yes | Zero | **Loses the feature.** No amber, no redteam, no persistence. Only viable as the fallback layer, which it already is |
| **D. Persist in a cookie and render `data-theme` server-side** | Requires a server | No flash | Yes | — | **Impossible.** GitHub Pages is static; there is no request-time rendering |
| **E. Set it from the deferred module** | The normal `type="module" defer` bundle sets `data-theme` after parsing | **Flash, guaranteed** | Yes | Zero | **Rejected.** This is the bug, not the fix |

**Implement option A.** It is the only one that is simultaneously flash-free, CSP-clean without
build-time hash gymnastics, and cacheable.

```html
<!-- theme/templates/base.html — inside <head>, as early as possible -->
<head>
  <meta charset="utf-8">
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover">
  <meta http-equiv="Content-Security-Policy" content="
    default-src 'self';
    script-src 'self' 'inline-speculation-rules';
    style-src 'self';
    img-src 'self' data:;
    media-src 'self';
    font-src 'self';
    connect-src 'self';
    base-uri 'none';
    form-action 'none';
    object-src 'none';
    frame-ancestors 'none'">
  <!-- 1. The theme boot: BLOCKING and FIRST. ~350 bytes. -->
  <script src="/js/theme-boot.js"></script>
  <!-- 2. Then the stylesheet, which is render-blocking anyway. -->
  <link rel="stylesheet" href="/css/styles.css">
  <!-- 3. Everything else is deferred. -->
  <script type="module" src="/js/main.js" defer></script>
</head>
```

Two important details about that `<meta>` CSP: `frame-ancestors` and `report-uri` are **ignored**
in meta-delivered policies (they require a real header), so note in your report that
clickjacking protection needs `X-Frame-Options`/`frame-ancestors` at the CDN or host level and
GitHub Pages does not provide it. Do not claim protection you do not have.

```js
// theme/static/js/theme-boot.js
// CLASSIC script (not a module), loaded BLOCKING in <head>.
// Its only job: set data-theme before first paint. Keep it under 500 bytes.
// It must not throw under any circumstance — a throw here delays the parser
// and leaves the page unthemed.
(function () {
  try {
    var t = localStorage.getItem('czr.theme');
    var valid = { green: 1, amber: 1, ice: 1, redteam: 1 };
    if (t && valid[t]) {
      document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-theme', t);
    }
    // No explicit choice: the CSS prefers-color-scheme rule handles it.
  } catch (e) {}
})();
```

Measure it and report it. It is the only blocking script on the site, so its size is a
first-class number:

```bash
gzip -c theme/static/js/theme-boot.js | wc -c
```

The trade-offs you must state honestly when you report:

- Option A costs **one additional HTTP request in the critical path**. Over HTTP/2 on the same
  connection with a warm cache this is typically sub-millisecond after the first visit, but on a
  cold first visit on a high-latency link it costs one round trip. That is the price of a
  strict CSP, and it is the right price to pay.
- The file must be served with a long `Cache-Control` and a content-hashed filename so the round
  trip is paid once. Check whether `build.py` hashes asset filenames; if not, that is a
  worthwhile proposal.
- **`styles.css` must not be loaded after a slow script.** Keep `theme-boot.js` tiny and first;
  if it ever grows, move logic out of it.

### 13.3 The toggle

```js
// theme/static/js/theme.js  (module, deferred — the boot script already ran)
const KEY = 'czr.theme';
const THEMES = ['green', 'amber', 'ice', 'redteam'];
const LABELS = { green: 'verde', amber: 'ámbar', ice: 'hielo', redteam: 'red team' };

export function currentTheme() {
  return document.documentElement.getAttribute('data-theme')
      || (matchMedia('(prefers-color-scheme: light)').matches ? 'ice' : 'green');
}

export function setTheme(name, { persist = true } = {}) {
  if (!THEMES.includes(name)) return;
  document.documentElement.setAttribute('data-theme', name);
  if (persist) { try { localStorage.setItem(KEY, name); } catch {} }
  document.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent('czr:theme', { detail: name }));
  // Keep the UA chrome (scrollbars, form controls) in step.
  document.querySelector('meta[name="theme-color"]')
    ?.setAttribute('content', getComputedStyle(document.documentElement)
      .getPropertyValue('--bg-page').trim());
}

// The terminal's `theme` command talks to us through an event, so the
// terminal never needs to import this module.
document.addEventListener('czr:set-theme', (e) => setTheme(e.detail));

// Cross-tab sync: changing the theme in one tab updates the others.
window.addEventListener('storage', (e) => {
  if (e.key === KEY && e.newValue) setTheme(e.newValue, { persist: false });
});

// The control itself: a real <button> cycling through the schemes, with the
// current value announced. A <select> is also acceptable and arguably better
// for discoverability; a bare icon that cycles silently is not.
const btn = document.querySelector('[data-theme-toggle]');
if (btn) {
  const sync = () => {
    const t = currentTheme();
    btn.setAttribute('aria-label', `Tema: ${LABELS[t]}. Cambiar al siguiente.`);
    btn.dataset.value = t;
  };
  btn.addEventListener('click', () => {
    const i = THEMES.indexOf(currentTheme());
    setTheme(THEMES[(i + 1) % THEMES.length]);
    sync();
    // Announce the change; the label change alone is not reliably announced.
    const live = document.querySelector('[data-live]');
    if (live) live.textContent = `Tema ${LABELS[currentTheme()]}`;
  });
  sync();
}
```

### 13.4 Theme switching must not animate

Do **not** put `transition: background-color 300ms` on `:root` or on everything. Switching a
theme repaints the entire page; adding a transition means repainting the entire page ~18 times
instead of once, and the intermediate colours are guaranteed to be non-compliant on contrast.
Switch instantly. If you want softness, transition **one** element (the toggle itself).

If the user asks for a crossfade, the correct mechanism is a same-document View Transition,
which snapshots and cross-fades on the compositor:

```js
function setThemeAnimated(name) {
  if (!document.startViewTransition || matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches) {
    return setTheme(name);
  }
  document.startViewTransition(() => setTheme(name));
}
```

That is one composited crossfade of two snapshots, not N repaints. It is the only acceptable
animated theme switch.

## 14. Typography and code presentation

### 14.1 The font stack

One self-hosted variable monospace as the primary face. Candidates that ship with an open
licence and a variable axis: **JetBrains Mono**, **IBM Plex Mono**, **Fira Code**, **Iosevka**,
**Recursive** (which has a `MONO` axis and a `CASL` axis — a genuinely interesting choice here
because it can be dialled between mechanical and warm).

Requirements the face must meet for this site:

- Full Latin-1 + Latin Extended-A coverage for Spanish: `á é í ó ú ü ñ Á É Í Ó Ú Ü Ñ ¿ ¡ «  »`.
- **Box-drawing characters** `U+2500–U+257F` for `tree` output and terminal chrome. Many
  otherwise-excellent faces do **not** include these — verify before committing to a face, or
  the terminal's tree output falls back to a different font mid-line and looks broken.
- Distinguishable `0/O`, `1/l/I`, `rn/m`. Non-negotiable for a security blog full of hashes and
  identifiers.
- A variable weight axis so you ship one file instead of four.

```css
:root {
  --font-mono: "CzrMono", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", "JetBrains Mono", "Cascadia Mono",
               "Menlo", "Consolas", "DejaVu Sans Mono", monospace;
  --font-ui: var(--font-mono);     /* the whole site is monospace, on purpose */
}
```

`ui-monospace` first among the fallbacks maps to the platform's own UI mono (SF Mono on macOS,
Cascadia/Consolas on Windows), which is metrically closer to a modern face than `monospace`
(often Courier) and therefore reflows less if the webfont fails.

### 14.2 Subsetting

Ship only what you use. A full variable mono is often 200–400 KB; a properly subset one for
ES+EN plus box drawing is typically 30–60 KB in `woff2`. Verify per file, do not quote a number
you did not measure.

Unicode ranges to keep:

| Range | Contents | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `U+0000-00FF` | Basic Latin + Latin-1 Supplement | ES accents, `¿ ¡ « » º ª ± × ÷` |
| `U+0100-017F` | Latin Extended-A | Occasional borrowed words, `œ`, `š` in references |
| `U+0192, U+02C6, U+02DC` | Assorted | Cheap, avoids tofu |
| `U+2000-206F` | General punctuation | `– — ' ' " " … ‰ †` |
| `U+2070-209F` | Super/subscripts | Footnote markers |
| `U+20A0-20BF` | Currency | `€` |
| `U+2100-214F` | Letterlike | `™ ℃ №` |
| `U+2190-21FF` | Arrows | `→ ← ↑ ↓ ⇒` used in prose and in the palette hints |
| `U+2200-22FF` | Math operators | `≤ ≥ ≠ ∈ ⊕ ⊗` in crypto writeups |
| `U+2500-257F` | **Box drawing** | `tree` output, terminal frames |
| `U+2580-259F` | Block elements | `█ ▄ ▀ ░ ▒ ▓` for the banner and the caret |

```bash
fi
```

Note `--layout-features`: dropping `calt` would kill programming ligatures if the face has them
(and you may *want* to drop them — ligatures like `!==` rendered as a single glyph are divisive
and can confuse copy-paste readers of exploit code; decide explicitly and note the decision).
`locl` matters for Spanish/Catalan forms. Never drop `ccmp` or `mark`: accents break.

### 14.3 Loading

```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "CzrMono";
  src: url("/fonts/czrmono-var.woff2") format("woff2-variations");
  font-weight: 300 700;          /* the variable range, one file */
  font-style: normal;
  font-display: swap;
  /* Repeat the subset ranges so the browser only downloads the file when a
     character in range is actually needed. With a single subset this is
     mostly documentation, but it becomes a real optimisation if you split
     the face into Latin and symbols. */
  unicode-range: U+0000-00FF, U+0100-017F, U+2000-206F, U+2190-21FF,
                 U+2500-257F, U+2580-259F, U+25A0-25FF;
}
```

`font-display: swap` means text paints immediately in the fallback and swaps when the webfont
arrives. The swap causes a reflow whose severity depends on how different the metrics are —
which is why the fallback stack matters and why you tune it:

```css
/* A metric-adjusted fallback: the browser scales the fallback face so its
   metrics match the webfont, making the swap nearly invisible. Compute the
   percentages by comparing the two faces' unitsPerEm, ascent, descent and
   average advance width — do not guess them.                              */
@font-face {
  font-family: "CzrMono Fallback";
  src: local("SF Mono"), local("Consolas"), local("DejaVu Sans Mono");
  size-adjust: 100%;          /* ← measure and set */
  ascent-override: 90%;       /* ← measure and set */
  descent-override: 22%;      /* ← measure and set */
  line-gap-override: 0%;
}
```

To compute the overrides, read both faces' `hhea`/`OS/2` tables:

```bash
python3 - <<'PY'
from fontTools.ttLib import TTFont
for path in ["CzrMono-Variable.ttf", "/usr/share/fonts/truetype/dejavu/DejaVuSansMono.ttf"]:
    f = TTFont(path)
    upm = f["head"].unitsPerEm
    hhea = f["hhea"]
    # advance width of a representative glyph
    adv = f["hmtx"]["A"][0] if "A" in f["hmtx"].metrics else None
    print(f"{path}\n  upm={upm} asc={hhea.ascent/upm:.4f} desc={abs(hhea.descent)/upm:.4f} "
          f"gap={hhea.lineGap/upm:.4f} advA={adv/upm if adv else None}")
PY
```

`size-adjust` = (webfont advance / fallback advance) × 100. Report the values you computed.

**Preload exactly the fonts used above the fold — and no more.** One file:

```html
<link rel="preload" href="/fonts/czrmono-var.woff2" as="font" type="font/woff2" crossorigin>
```

`crossorigin` is mandatory on font preloads even for same-origin fonts, because fonts are always
fetched in CORS mode; without it the browser downloads the file **twice**. This is one of the
most common performance bugs on the web. Verify in the Network panel: exactly one request for
the font file.

Never preload more than one font file. Every preload competes with the LCP resource for
bandwidth in the critical window.

Requirements, each with its implementation:

**Line numbers that do not break copy-paste.** Never put the number in the DOM as text. Use a
CSS counter or a `data-line` attribute rendered via `::before`, because pseudo-element content
is not included in the selection:

```css
.code__code { counter-reset: line; }

  -webkit-user-select: none;
}
```

Verify by selecting three lines and pasting into a text editor: you must get the code and no
numbers.

**Horizontal scroll containment.**

```css
.code__pre {
  overflow-x: auto;
  overscroll-behavior-x: contain;    /* no page-level back-navigation gesture */
  /* Never let a long line widen the article column. */
  max-inline-size: 100%;

```

**Long-token wrapping.** Base64 blobs and 200-character URLs make horizontal scrolling useless.
Offer an opt-in wrap toggle rather than wrapping everything (wrapping code by default destroys
column alignment in terminal output):

```css

  padding-inline-start: 4ch;

```

**Line highlighting.** A background on the line element, plus a left border. Do not use
`box-shadow` and do not animate it.


**The copy button.**

```js
// theme/static/js/code/copy.js
for (const fig of document.querySelectorAll('[data-code]')) {
  const btn = fig.querySelector('[data-code-copy]');
  const code = fig.querySelector('code');
  if (!btn || !code) continue;
  btn.hidden = false;                 // ships hidden: no JS, no dead button

  const original = btn.textContent;
  let resetTimer = 0;

  btn.addEventListener('click', async () => {
    // innerText, not textContent: it respects line breaks from block-level
    // spans and does not include pseudo-element line numbers.
    const text = code.innerText;
    let ok = true;
    try {
      await navigator.clipboard.writeText(text);
    } catch {
      ok = false;
    }
    btn.textContent = ok ? 'copiado' : 'error';
    btn.dataset.state = ok ? 'ok' : 'err';
    // Announce it: a label change on a button the user just pressed is not
    // reliably read. A polite live region is.
    const live = document.querySelector('[data-live]');
    if (live) live.textContent = ok
      ? 'Código copiado al portapapeles'
      : 'No se pudo copiar; selecciona el texto manualmente';
    clearTimeout(resetTimer);
    resetTimer = setTimeout(() => {
      btn.textContent = original;
      delete btn.dataset.state;
    }, 2000);
  });
}
```

The live region lives once in the base template:

```html
<p class="u-visually-hidden" role="status" aria-live="polite" data-live></p>
```

`navigator.clipboard` requires a secure context. On `http://localhost` it works; on plain
`http://` on a LAN IP it does not — so the failure path above is real and must be implemented,
not assumed away.

**Token colours in four themes.** Every syntax token class needs a value in every theme, and
every one must pass 4.5:1 against `--bg-surface` *and* against the highlighted-line background.
Use a small, disciplined token set — 6 categories, not 30:

```css
:root {
  --tok-comment: var(--fg-subtle);
  --tok-keyword: var(--fg-accent);
  --tok-string:  var(--color-ok);
  --tok-number:  var(--color-warn);
  --tok-func:    var(--fg-primary);
  --tok-punct:   var(--fg-muted);
}
```

Comments are the pair that fails: designers always make them the dimmest colour on the page,
and comments in exploit code are *content*, not decoration. `--fg-subtle` at ≥ 5:1 is the floor;
do not go below it "because comments should recede".

Check every token in every theme:

```bash

```

where `token-pairs.txt` lists `#fg #bg label` triples, one per line, generated once per theme.

## 15. Responsive and input-modality behaviour

### 15.1 Breakpoint strategy

Content-driven, not device-driven. Four points, named after what changes:

| Token | Width | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| `--bp-sm` | `30rem` (480 px) | Single column stops being cramped; the terminal toolbar can show more keys |
| `--bp-md` | `48rem` (768 px) | Two-column card grid; the header shows the palette button with its label |
| `--bp-lg` | `64rem` (1024 px) | The article gets a sticky TOC sidebar |
| `--bp-xl` | `80rem` (1280 px) | Max content width reached; margins grow, layout does not |

Custom properties cannot be used in media query conditions, so define the breakpoints as
`@custom-media` if you preprocess, or simply write the values and keep them in a comment block
at the top of `layout.css`. Do not scatter magic widths through the codebase.

Everything between breakpoints is handled by `clamp()` and intrinsic sizing, so there are only
four of them and each one earns its place.

### 15.2 Fluid type and space

Already specified in §3.4/§3.5. The rule that keeps it accessible: **`clamp()` must never
prevent zooming.** A `font-size` whose maximum is expressed only in `vw` does not respond to the
user's browser zoom or default font size. Every `clamp()` in the type scale therefore has `rem`
in its `min` and `max` and only a small `vw` term in the middle. Test by setting the browser's
default font size to 24 px: all text must grow.

Also verify WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.4 (Resize Text): at 200 % zoom, no loss of content or functionality;
and SC 1.4.10 (Reflow): at 320 CSS px width (i.e. 1280 px at 400 % zoom), no two-dimensional
scrolling. Test this by setting the viewport to 320 × 512 in DevTools and reading a full article.

### 15.3 Container queries

Cards appear in the article grid, in the sidebar, and in the palette. A media query cannot know
which. A container query can.

```css
.card-grid { container-type: inline-size; container-name: grid; }

.post-card { display: grid; gap: var(--space-3); }

@container grid (min-width: 34rem) {
  .post-card {
    grid-template-columns: minmax(0, 1fr) auto;
    align-items: start;
  }
  .post-card__meta { text-align: end; }
}
```

`container-type: inline-size` establishes containment on the inline axis, which is also a
performance win (it implies `layout` and `style` containment). Do not set `container-type: size`
unless the element has a fixed block size — it will collapse.

Container query units (`cqi`, `cqb`) are useful for component-internal type scaling:

```css
.post-card__title { font-size: clamp(1rem, 0.9rem + 1.2cqi, 1.375rem); }
```

### 15.4 Touch targets and pointer capability

```css
/* WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) is 24x24 CSS px. Apple's and
   Google's guidance is 44/48. On coarse pointers, use 44.                  */
:where(button, [role="button"], a.button, .term__key) {
  min-block-size: 44px;
  min-inline-size: 44px;
  display: inline-grid;
  place-items: center;

  /* A mouse is precise; dense toolbars are fine, but never below 24px.     */
  :where(.toolbar button) { min-block-size: 28px; min-inline-size: 28px; }
}

/* Hover styles ONLY where hover exists. Without this, a tap on a touch
   device leaves the element in a stuck :hover state until the next tap
   elsewhere — the single most common "why is this card still glowing" bug. */
@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine) {
  .post-card:hover { --card-glow: var(--glow-2); }
}

/* Safe-area insets for notched phones. Requires viewport-fit=cover.        */
.site-header { padding-inline: max(var(--gutter), env(safe-area-inset-left)); }
.site-footer { padding-block-end: max(var(--space-6), env(safe-area-inset-bottom)); }
.term { padding-block-end: max(var(--space-3), env(safe-area-inset-bottom)); }

/* Dynamic viewport units: `vh` on mobile is the WRONG height because the
   URL bar collapses. Use dvh for anything that should fill the screen.     */
.boot { block-size: 100dvh; }
```

### 15.5 Every keyboard feature needs a touch equivalent

This is a hard rule, and it is the one most often broken by "power user" sites.

| Keyboard feature | Touch equivalent | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `Ctrl+K` palette | A visible search button in the header with an accessible name, plus the `⌘K` hint shown only on `@media (pointer: fine)` | `[data-palette-open]` |
| Terminal `Tab` completion | A `Tab` key button in the coarse-pointer toolbar | `.term__keys` |
| Terminal `↑`/`↓` history | `↑`/`↓` buttons in the same toolbar | `.term__keys` |
| Terminal `Ctrl+L` clear | A `clear` chip, or the `clear` command is discoverable via `help` | `.term__keys` |
| `Escape` to close a dialog | A visible close button, ≥ 44 px, first in the tab order | every dialog |
| `/` to search | Same header button | — |

```css
/* The key toolbar exists only where there is no physical keyboard.        */

@media (pointer: coarse) {
  .term__keys { display: flex; gap: var(--space-2); flex-wrap: wrap; }
}
/* And the keyboard hint exists only where there is one.                    */
.kbd-hint { display: none; }
@media (pointer: fine) { .kbd-hint { display: inline; } }
```

Detect the platform for the modifier label without user-agent sniffing where possible:

```js

  || /Mac|iPhone|iPad/.test(navigator.platform || '');
for (const el of document.querySelectorAll('[data-mod-key]')) {
  el.textContent = isMac ? '⌘' : 'Ctrl';
}
```

## 16. Anti-patterns gallery

Each entry: the broken version, why it is broken, and the fixed version. When you review

### A1 — Animating `box-shadow` instead of `opacity` on a pseudo-element

```css
/* ❌ Repaints the card and its blurred shadow bounds on every frame of the
   transition. On a grid of 12 cards, hovering across them repaints
   continuously. `box-shadow` blur is a large-radius raster operation.     */

```

```css
/* ✅ The shadow is painted ONCE on a pseudo-element and never changes; only

  content: "";
  position: absolute;
  inset: 0;

  pointer-events: none;
  transition: opacity var(--dur-fast) var(--ease-out);
```

### A2 — A per-component `requestAnimationFrame` loop

```js
// ❌ Six components, six rAF loops, six callbacks per frame, none of which
//    stop when the tab is hidden or the element scrolls away. Each one
//    reads layout, so the frame ends with six forced reflows.
function tick() {
  el.style.transform = `translateY(${el.getBoundingClientRect().top * 0.1}px)`;
  requestAnimationFrame(tick);
}
tick();
```

```js
// ✅ One driver, capped rate, paused off-screen and when hidden, and no
//    layout read inside the frame (the position comes from the observer).
import { subscribe } from '../core/scheduler.js';
import { whileVisible } from '../core/visible.js';

let offset = 0;
new IntersectionObserver(([e]) => { offset = e.boundingClientRect.top; },
  { threshold: [0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1] }).observe(el);

let handle = null;
whileVisible(el, {
  onEnter: () => { handle = subscribe(() => {
    el.style.transform = `translate3d(0, ${offset * 0.1}px, 0)`;
  }, { fps: 30 }); },
  onLeave: () => { handle?.stop(); handle = null; },
});
```

Find them:

```bash
grep -rn "requestAnimationFrame" /home/kali/Desktop/CzrXplo1t/theme/static/js/ \
  | grep -v "core/scheduler.js"
```

Every hit outside the scheduler needs a justification, and "it is only one small loop" is not
one. (`requestAnimationFrame` used *once*, not in a loop — e.g. to defer a layout write by a
frame — is fine; the anti-pattern is the self-perpetuating loop.)

### A6 — An infinite flicker over the photosensitivity threshold

```css
/* ❌ ~8 luminance transitions per second across a large area. This is not
   a style choice, it is a seizure risk and a WCAG 2.3.1 (Level A) failure. */
@keyframes flicker {
  0%, 19%, 21%, 23%, 25%, 54%, 56%, 100% { opacity: 1; }
  20%, 22%, 24%, 55% { opacity: 0.3; }
}
.crt { animation: flicker 1.2s infinite; }
```

```css
/* ✅ Two transitions per four seconds, 3% amplitude, and it stops entirely
   for reduced motion. Reads as "alive", cannot harm anyone.               */
@keyframes fx-tube-idle { from { opacity: 0.97; } to { opacity: 1; } }
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) {
  .fx-crt { animation: fx-tube-idle 4s var(--ease-inout) infinite alternate; }
}
```

Audit rule: for any `infinite` animation, count the number of keyframe stops that change
luminance by more than ~10 %, divide by the duration, and require **< 3 per second** — and
prefer **< 1 per second** for anything covering more than a small area.

### A10 — Layout thrash: read/write/read/write in a loop

```js
// ❌ Each `offsetHeight` read forces the engine to flush the pending style
//    and layout changes from the previous write. N elements = N layouts.
for (const el of items) {
  el.style.height = 'auto';
  const h = el.offsetHeight;      // forced synchronous layout
  el.style.height = h + 'px';
}
```

```js
// ✅ Batch: read everything, then write everything. One layout total.
const heights = items.map(el => { el.style.height = 'auto'; return 0; });
// force one layout after all the writes:
items.forEach((el, i) => { heights[i] = el.offsetHeight; });
items.forEach((el, i) => { el.style.height = heights[i] + 'px'; });
```

Better still: do not measure. `height: auto` with `grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr` animates an
accordion open with no measurement at all.

### 17.4 `PerformanceObserver` for LCP and CLS

Drop this into a temporary module while measuring. **Remove it before shipping** — it is not
part of the site's byte budget and the site has no analytics.

```js
// tools/perf-probe.js — TEMPORARY. Never ship.
(() => {
  let lcp = 0, cls = 0;

  new PerformanceObserver((list) => {
    for (const e of list.getEntries()) {
      lcp = e.startTime;
      console.log('[LCP]', Math.round(e.startTime), 'ms', e.element);
    }
  }).observe({ type: 'largest-contentful-paint', buffered: true });

  new PerformanceObserver((list) => {
    for (const e of list.getEntries()) {
      if (e.hadRecentInput) continue;     // user-initiated shifts do not count
      cls += e.value;
      console.log('[CLS] +', e.value.toFixed(4), '→', cls.toFixed(4), e.sources?.map(s => s.node));
    }
  }).observe({ type: 'layout-shift', buffered: true });

  new PerformanceObserver((list) => {
    for (const e of list.getEntries()) {
      console.log('[LongTask]', Math.round(e.duration), 'ms', e.attribution?.[0]?.name);
    }
  }).observe({ type: 'longtask', buffered: true });

  // INP proxy: the slowest interaction observed.
  let worst = 0;
  new PerformanceObserver((list) => {
    for (const e of list.getEntries()) {
      if (e.duration > worst) {
        worst = e.duration;
        console.log('[INP worst]', Math.round(e.duration), 'ms', e.name, e.target);
      }
    }
  }).observe({ type: 'event', buffered: true, durationThreshold: 40 });

  addEventListener('pagehide', () => {
    console.log('FINAL  LCP:', Math.round(lcp), 'ms  CLS:', cls.toFixed(4), ' INP~:', Math.round(worst), 'ms');
  });
})();
```

Targets: **LCP < 1.5 s** on a local build (it is a static page with no blocking JS, so anything
worse means you broke something), **CLS = 0** (not "< 0.1" — zero; there is no excuse for shift
on a static site), **no long task > 50 ms** during load, **INP < 200 ms**.

## 20. Working method

### 20.1 Before you touch anything

1. Read the relevant existing files. Do not assume the structure from this document — this
   document describes the *target*, and the repo may be at an earlier stage.
2. Run the build once, unchanged, so you have a baseline that works and a baseline set of
   numbers.
3. Measure the baseline: JS bytes, CSS bytes, and (if a browser is available) LCP/CLS.
4. Identify which invariant the request is closest to violating, and decide up front how you
   will keep it.

### 20.2 While you work

- Change one thing at a time. A change that mixes a new effect with a token refactor cannot be
  measured or reverted cleanly.
- Add tokens before components. If your component needs a value that has no token, add the
  token in `tokens.css` for all four themes first.
- Write the reduced-motion and no-JS paths **at the same time** as the main path, not
  afterwards. Retrofitted degradation is always worse and usually forgotten.
- Prefer deleting to adding. The best-performing version of any effect is the one you decided
  not to ship. If you can remove an existing effect while making the page better, propose it.

### 20.3 When the request conflicts with an invariant

Say so immediately, in the report, and offer the closest compliant alternative. Examples of the
form:

> *No puedo añadir GSAP (invariante I1: cero dependencias de terceros). Lo que sí puedo hacer es
> escribir el timeline concreto que necesitas con la Web Animations API en ~40 líneas, que es la
> parte de GSAP que este efecto usa. Coste estimado: 1.1 KB gzip.*

> *Un `<script>` inline en `<head>` para el tema está prohibido por el CSP (invariante I2). La
> alternativa sin parpadeo es un script externo bloqueante de ~350 B; ver §13.2 para el
> análisis de las cinco opciones.*

> *El GIF de 9 MB rompe I7 por un factor de 4,5. Convertido a WebM debería quedar en torno a
> 400 KB — pero `ffmpeg` no está instalado, así que no lo he tocado y no puedo darte el número
> real hasta que lo instales.*

Never quietly break an invariant to satisfy a request. Never quietly drop a request to satisfy
an invariant without telling the user.

### 20.4 The final question

Before you write the report, re-read §0.1 and answer it honestly. If you would be embarrassed by
a performance profile, an accessibility audit, a JS-disabled load or a reduced-motion load, go
back and fix it — and if you cannot fix it, say exactly that in **No medido / pendiente**.
